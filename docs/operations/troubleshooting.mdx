---
title: Troubleshooting
description: Real symptoms, what actually causes them, and where to look in this installation
---

Start with `/readyz` on the processes that serve it, and with the `omniflow_dependency_up`
gauge. Most incidents are one dependency, and the rest of this page assumes you have
already ruled that out.

Two things about readiness are worth knowing before you rely on it:

- The **bot** serves no HTTP at all unless `APP_BOT_METRICS_ADDR` is set, and that
  address is ignored when it equals `APP_BOT_HTTP_ADDR` — publishing probes and metrics
  on the internet-facing webhook host is refused. Out of the box the bot has no probe
  endpoint, so "the bot is not answering `/readyz`" usually means it was never listening.
- An **API** started without `APP_DATABASE_URL` registers no dependency probes at all and
  answers `200 {"status":"ready","checks":[]}`. That is a green light for a process that
  can serve only the probes and `/metrics`.

The worker registers `postgres` only. The API registers `postgres`, `valkey`, and
`remnawave` when commerce is configured. See
[Observability](/operations/observability).

## A customer paid and has no access

This is the failure Omniflow is designed hardest against, and it is almost always
provisioning rather than payment.

1. Find the order. `GET /v1/admin/orders/{orderID}` shows its state and paid amount.
2. `paid` means the money is recorded and the entitlement exists. `fulfilled` means
   Remnawave confirmed it.
3. `GET /v1/admin/jobs?state=dead_letter` shows provisioning runs that will not retry on
   their own.
4. `GET /v1/admin/fulfillment/drifts` shows entitlements whose remote state does not match.

To re-drive a stalled run, `POST /v1/admin/jobs/{jobID}/retry`, or from the panel
`/admin/system` → Jobs. The underlying fulfillment operation carries its own idempotency
key, so a retry cannot double-apply something that already succeeded.

The payment is never at risk here. Settlement and the durable job commit in one
transaction, so a paid order always has a job waiting for it.

## The worker exits at start-up complaining about a missing table

The `migrate` service applies the committed Atlas migrations in `database/migrations`.
Those migrations create Omniflow's own schema; they do not create the queue's internal
tables (`river_job`, `river_leader`, `river_client`, `river_queue`, `river_migration`),
and no process in the stack creates them either. If the worker fails at
`client.Start` reporting one of those relations missing, that is the cause rather than a
broken database URL or a partly applied migration.

Until those tables exist, the worker cannot start, and a checkout that inserts a
fulfillment job inside its settlement transaction fails with it. Check the worker's
stderr for the relation name before assuming a connectivity problem. See
[Jobs and queues](/operations/jobs-and-queues).

## A payment succeeded at the provider but the order is pending

The webhook has not arrived, or it was rejected.

1. `GET /v1/admin/webhooks` lists recent intake with `signatureValid` and `status`.
2. `signatureValid: false` means the signature did not verify. Check the provider
   credentials, and confirm the reverse proxy is not modifying the request body — a
   re-encoded body invalidates every signature, because the signature is computed over
   the exact bytes the provider sent.
3. `status: failed` with `payment_not_found` means the webhook referenced a payment intent
   this installation does not have. Usually a webhook from a different environment
   pointed at this one.
4. If no webhook arrived at all, `POST /v1/admin/payments/{paymentID}/reconcile` polls the
   provider directly. The API also re-polls up to 100 in-flight intents every minute on
   its own.

Both paths are idempotent. A webhook arriving after a manual reconciliation is a no-op.

<Note>
  The panel's own **Reconcile** and **Refund** buttons on `/admin/finance` record an
  audit event for who asked and why; they do not themselves re-poll the provider or move
  money. Use the `/v1/admin` routes above when you need the provider contacted.
</Note>

## Provider credentials look right in the panel and payments still fail

The payment adapters the API and worker call are built from environment variables at
start-up — `APP_TELEGRAM_TOKEN`, `APP_CRYPTOBOT_TOKEN`, `APP_YOOKASSA_SHOP_ID`,
`APP_YOOKASSA_SECRET`. The credentials stored on the panel's provider cards are not what
the running process uses. Change the variable and restart. See
[Payments](/integrations/payments) and
[Installation settings](/operations/installation-settings).

## A top-up credited the wrong amount

Look at the `topup` topic or the payment events for the order. A top-up credits exactly
what the provider settled:

- `overpayment` — more arrived than was requested, and all of it was credited.
- `underpayment` — less arrived, and that is what was credited.

Both leave the order `paid`. This is intended: a wallet owes nothing beyond the money
itself. If the discrepancy needs correcting, use a wallet adjustment with a reason, which
is recorded as a compensating ledger entry rather than a rewrite.

## A customer is refused a top-up they think is within the limits

Top-up limits come from `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_*` on the API and bot, not from the top-up card
in the panel. Saving new figures on that card changes what the card shows and not what
the store enforces. Check the environment values and the rolling-window sum: the refusal
is usually `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_WINDOW_LIMIT_MINOR` over `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_WINDOW_HOURS`
rather than the single-transaction ceiling.

## A saved cart is not buying itself

Check `carts.last_failure`:

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `cart_insufficient_balance` | Working as intended; the balance does not cover the re-validated total |
| `cart_plan_unavailable` | The plan or an add-on was archived, retired, or lost its price |
| `cart_price_changed` | The order was refused at the current price |
| `cart_auto_purchase_off` | The customer turned automatic purchase off |
| `cart_maintenance` | The installation is in maintenance |

Remember the cart is re-priced every attempt. A cart saved before a price rise will not
buy at the old price, by design.

## Duplicate charges

They should be impossible, and the invariants say why:

- `orders (user_id, idempotency_key)` is unique, so one checkout resolves to one order.
- `ledger_transactions.idempotency_key` is unique and `(reference_type, reference_id, type)`
  is unique, so one settlement credits once.
- `entitlement_addons (order_id, addon_version_id)` is unique, so one add-on applies once.
- `fulfillment_operations.idempotency_key` is unique, so one operation runs once.

If you believe you are seeing a duplicate, compare `ledger_entries` for the customer. Two
entries under one transaction are the two sides of one movement, not two charges.

## The operator group is silent

1. Confirm `APP_OPERATOR_CHAT_ID` is set and the bot was restarted. With it unset the bot
   logs `operator notifications are disabled because no group is configured` and never
   drains the queue — notices still accumulate as `pending` rows and are delivered once a
   group is configured.
2. Check `operator_topics` for a row with `status = 'failed'` and read `last_error_code`.
   See [Operator notifications](/operations/operator-notifications).
3. Check `operator_notifications` for rows with `status = 'suppressed'` and
   `error_code = 'volume_cap'`, which means the cap is doing its job.
4. If only *some* topics are quiet, that may be correct. Purchases, renewals, top-ups,
   refunds, fulfillment failures, and admin security all have producers. The 🚨 Incidents
   and 💾 Backups topics are created but nothing currently queues into them.

## An operator cannot sign in and the page says "rate limited"

Sign-in rate limiting is backed by Valkey and **fails closed**: when Valkey is
unreachable the limiter answers "not allowed" rather than waving requests through, so a
Valkey outage blocks operator sign-in entirely. Check the `valkey` entry in the API's
`/readyz` output first.

A genuine limit answers `429 rate_limited`; a locked account answers `429 account_locked`
after five consecutive failures, with the wait doubling up to one hour.

Note that a Valkey outage does **not** open maintenance mode. Automatic detection watches
`postgres` and `remnawave` only, so `valkey` appears in `/readyz` and never triggers a
window on its own.

## The bot receives no updates

In long-polling mode, check the bot's logs for Telegram API errors and confirm outbound
HTTPS works.

In webhook mode:

1. Confirm the proxy routes `POST /telegram/webhook` to the bot's listener
   (`APP_BOT_HTTP_ADDR`, default `:8082`) and does not rewrite the body. The endpoint caps
   bodies at 1 MiB.
2. Confirm `APP_TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK_SECRET` matches what was registered. An update without
   the secret token header is discarded before parsing, which looks identical to no
   traffic. The secret must match `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{32,256}$` or the bot refuses to start.
3. `APP_TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK_URL` must be HTTPS, or the bot refuses to start.
4. Ask Telegram what it thinks: `getWebhookInfo` reports the URL it is delivering to and
   the last error it saw.

Switching back to long polling is a supported fallback: clear `APP_TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK_URL`
and restart.

## `/ops` does nothing in Telegram

The command is always registered and answers **nothing at all** to an account that may
not use it, so the command's existence is not confirmed to a customer. Two conditions
must both hold before you get any reply:

1. `APP_OPERATOR_TELEGRAM_IDS` contains the Telegram account's numeric ID, and the bot has
   been restarted since. An empty list disables the surface entirely, which is the
   default.
2. The message is sent in a **private** chat with the bot. In a group it is ignored.

If you do get a reply and it reads *"Backups are disabled"*, the surface is working and
`APP_BACKUP_ENABLED` or `APP_BACKUP_ENCRYPTION_KEY` is missing. A malformed entry in
`APP_OPERATOR_TELEGRAM_IDS` is a fatal configuration error, so check the bot's start-up
log if the process exited immediately instead.

## Purchases are refused with a maintenance notice

Check `GET /v1/admin/maintenance` or the panel's `/admin/system` → Health tab. `source`
says whether it was manual (`manual`) or automatic (`database`, `remnawave`). An automatic
window clears itself once the dependency has been healthy for
`APP_MAINTENANCE_RECOVERY_STREAK` consecutive samples; a manual one never clears itself.

A fulfillment job that meets an active window is snoozed for a minute rather than failed,
and its attempt count is given back, so a maintenance hold costs no retry budget. See
[Maintenance mode](/operations/maintenance-mode).

## The pending outbox count only goes up

It always will. `outbox_events` rows are written on `order.paid`, `wallet.credited`, and
`addon.purchased`, and nothing in the product marks them published. `GET /v1/admin/outbox`
and the panel's outbox tab therefore report a number that grows for the life of the
installation, and `APP_RETENTION_OUTBOX_DAYS` — which only deletes rows that have been
published — removes nothing.

It is not a backlog, it is not a stalled dispatcher, and no action clears it. Do not alert
on it. The declared `omniflow_outbox_pending_events` gauge is never written either, so
there is nothing to scrape.

## Backups are failing

The failure reason is on the backup row and in the log of the process that took it, as a
classified code:

| Code | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| `command_exit_N` | `pg_dump` or `pg_restore` exited non-zero, usually credentials or a version mismatch |
| `verification_failed` | The written file could not be read back or its digest did not match; check disk health and free space |
| `file_missing` | The backup directory is not writable or not mounted |
| `cancelled` | The process shut down mid-backup |
| `backup_failed` | Anything else the service could not classify |

Scheduled backups run in the **worker**; `/ops` backups and restores run in the **bot**,
so look in that process's log even though the on-screen message mentions the worker.

`pg_dump` must match the server major version. The bot and worker images ship the
PostgreSQL 18 client tools for exactly this reason; if you run them elsewhere, point
`APP_PG_DUMP_PATH` and `APP_PG_RESTORE_PATH` at matching binaries. See
[Backup and restore](/operations/backup-restore).

## A support attachment uploaded from the web is gone

`APP_SUPPORT_ATTACHMENT_DIR` (default `/var/lib/omniflow/support-attachments`) is read by
both the API, which writes attachments, and the worker, which reclaims their files during
retention. Two failure shapes follow:

- The compose stack mounts no volume at that path on either service, so an attachment
  written by the API lives in the container's writable layer and is lost when the
  container is rebuilt. Mount a volume on the API if browser-uploaded attachments must
  survive a redeploy.
- If the worker's value differs from the API's, retention deletes the rows and leaves the
  bytes on disk forever. Set the same path on both.

Attachments in Telegram-only installations are references to files Telegram holds and are
unaffected.

## Everything happens twice

Every scheduled loop — maintenance detection, payment reconciliation, the order and cart
sweep, renewals, retention, campaigns, bulk actions, channel re-checks, scheduled backups,
operator notification dispatch — is an in-process ticker with no leader election. Running
two APIs runs the maintenance controller and the reconciler twice; two workers run every
sweep twice; two bots dispatch operator notifications twice. Run one instance of each
role. The durable job queue is the exception: it elects a leader of its own.

## A process is down and nothing brought it back

The bundled `compose.yaml` declares no `restart:` policy on `postgres`, `valkey`, `api`,
`bot`, `worker`, or `web` — only the Traefik overlay sets one. A crashed process stays
down until somebody notices. Add a restart policy for an unattended deployment. See
[Deployment](/operations/deployment).

## A customer has two subscriptions and expects one

Concurrent subscriptions are off by default. If a customer has more than one, either the
switch is on, or they were adopted from separate Remnawave users at import. See
[Subscriptions](/commerce/subscriptions) for how to reduce a customer to one without
orphaning access.

## Traces are empty

Omniflow opens spans across inbound HTTP, Telegram updates, fulfillment jobs, PostgreSQL
queries, and outbound HTTP, but it never installs a tracer provider and reads no `OTEL_*`
variable. With no provider installed the global one is a no-op and no span is exported,
so setting collector variables alone changes nothing. Metrics and logs are the
instrumentation you can rely on today.
