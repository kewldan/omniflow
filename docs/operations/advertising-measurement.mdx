`/admin/settings/analytics` turns on measurement of your own storefront, and
`/admin/reports` → **Channels** shows what it found.

Two things before anything else.

**This is your analytics, not the project's.** Nothing here is reported to the
Omniflow project or to anybody else. It is separate from
[telemetry](/operations/telemetry), which is the project's and is also off by
default, and the two are deliberately different screens so neither is mistaken
for the other.

**Nothing is uploaded to an advertising network by this software.** The
conversion export is a file you download and hand to the platform yourself.
Sending a customer's purchase to Google because a settings toggle was on is not
something anybody should have to discover from behaviour.

## Why a counter alone attributes nothing

A counter script watches the storefront. It sees the visit.

It never sees the sale. Payment here happens on the backend — often a day after
the click, sometimes through a transfer you confirm by hand — and by then the
browser session that carried the advertising platform's click identifier is
long gone. The platform sold you a click tagged `gclid` or `yclid` and has no
way of learning that the click turned into money.

That is why the roadmap said *no advertising channel can be attributed at all*,
and it was true even for an installation that had pasted a counter in.

So the identifier is carried:

1. A visitor arrives on a link carrying `gclid`, `yclid`, `fbclid`, `msclkid`,
   `ttclid`, `twclid`, or `utm_*` parameters.
2. If they have agreed to measurement, the browser holds those parameters
   first-party for thirty days. First write wins — somebody who lands from an
   advertisement, thinks for a week, and returns through a friend's link was
   brought here by the advertisement.
3. When they confirm a purchase, the parameters are attached to **that order**.
4. When the order settles, it appears in the export with its click identifier
   and its amount.

The attachment is to the order, not to the customer. An order is a conversion
with an amount and a date, which is exactly what an offline upload takes. A
click identifier held against a person for the life of their account is a
profile — a different thing, with different obligations, that none of this
needs.

## A counter is a number, never a snippet

There is no field here to paste counter code into, and that is deliberate.

A textarea that accepted a script would be a way to run arbitrary code in every
customer's browser, gated only by an operator write permission — and a
customer's browser holds subscription links, which are credentials. So the
script that ends up on the page is written in this repository and the only thing
you supply is an identifier, validated against that provider's shape:

| Provider | Identifier |
| --- | --- |
| Yandex Metrica | The counter number, 5–12 digits |
| Google Analytics 4 | The measurement ID, `G-` and 6–16 characters |

It is less flexible than a snippet field. What that flexibility costs is the
thing it gives up.

**Enabled is separate from having identifiers.** Turning measurement off in a
hurry — a regulator asked, a customer complained — should not mean finding the
numbers again to turn it back on, so the identifiers stay and nothing renders.

## Consent

Counters load only for a visitor who agreed to them, and so does the click
capture. A visitor who declines is not measured, and whatever their landing page
carried is forgotten rather than held waiting for a purchase.

The request appears only when there is something to agree to. An installation
that has configured no counter shows nobody a banner: asking somebody to agree
to nothing is worse than not asking.

Both buttons carry the same weight. A design where declining is a small grey
link beside a large coloured accept collects a consent nobody gave, and an
installation selling privacy software is the last place that should ship one.

## Site verification

`<meta name="google-site-verification">` and its equivalents prove to a
webmaster tool that you control the domain. They are **not** counters: they set
no cookie, load no script, and observe nobody.

So they render for every visitor, regardless of the measurement switch and
regardless of consent. A verification tag that only appeared after somebody
consented would verify nothing — the thing fetching the page is a tool, not a
person who can consent.

Four are supported: `google-site-verification`, `yandex-verification`,
`msvalidate.01`, and `facebook-domain-verification`. The name comes from that
allowlist and the content must be an opaque token; "any meta tag an operator
wants" is a much larger feature than "prove you own this domain".

## The channel report and the export

`/admin/reports` → **Channels**, over the same period as the sales report and
keyed the same way: on when the money arrived.

| Column | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Channel | The `utm_source`, or the platform that issued the click identifier, or `direct` |
| Orders | Settled orders attributed to that channel |
| Uploadable | How many of those carry a click identifier |
| Revenue | What was paid |
| Refunded | What came back |

**Uploadable is the column worth reading.** An order is attributed to a channel
as soon as it carried a UTM tag, but only an order carrying the platform's own
click identifier can be matched back to the click you paid for. Twenty orders
and three uploadable ones means the links are losing the identifier somewhere —
usually a redirect that strips the query — and that is worth knowing before
anybody concludes the advertising is not working.

**Export for upload** downloads a CSV in the column set an offline-conversion
import expects:

```
order_id,click_id,click_source,conversion_time,state,currency,value_minor,refunded_minor,utm_source,utm_medium,utm_campaign,utm_content,utm_term
```

Refunded orders are in it, with their refunded amount, rather than being
dropped. A platform that optimised bidding on a sale that was later returned is
being taught the wrong thing, and you can only correct for that if the export
says it happened.

## What is deliberately absent

- **No server-side conversion API calls.** Nothing here reaches an advertising
  network. Adding that would mean this software sending a customer's purchase to
  a third party on a schedule, and that decision belongs to you and to a file you
  hand over knowingly.
- **No cross-visit identity.** Nothing joins two visits by the same person. The
  click is attached to one order and forgotten.
- **No measurement in the operator panel.** Nobody advertises their way to an
  admin login, and an operator is not a person to be measured.

## Permissions

| Action | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| Read the configuration | `settings.read` |
| Change it | `settings.write` |
| Read the channel report and the export | `finance.read` |

`finance.read` rather than a permission of its own: a conversion export is
revenue data with an advertising label on it, and the person who reads the sales
report is the person who reads this.

## Reference

| Route | Method | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/v1/analytics` | `GET` | Public. What a visitor's browser renders; `measurable` false means nothing would run even with consent |
| `/v1/panel/analytics` | `GET` | `settings.read` |
| `/v1/panel/analytics` | `PUT` | `settings.write`, with the current `version` |
| `/v1/panel/reports/channels` | `GET` | `finance.read`; `?since&until` |
| `/v1/panel/reports/conversions` | `GET` | `finance.read`; `?since&until&source&format=csv` |
| `/v1/account/orders/{id}/attribution` | `POST` | The customer's own order; an order that is not theirs answers 404 |
