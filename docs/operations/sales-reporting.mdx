---
title: Sales reporting
description: What was sold over a period you choose, why the dashboard's window stays fixed, and the three rules that decide which figures are ever added together
---

`/admin/reports` answers the questions the dashboard cannot: how a quarter went,
which plan is actually selling, how much of the money is renewals rather than new
business, and whether the trials turn into anything.

The dashboard's thirty-day window stays exactly where it is. A window that moves
cannot be compared between two visits, which is the property an operations
overview needs and the reason it was fixed in the first place. This is the screen
where the window moves.

## Three rules that decide the numbers

**The period keys on when the order was paid, and that instant never moves.**
`orders.paid_at` is written the first time an order reaches a settled state and
never again. Before this existed, reporting keyed on `updated_at` — so recording
a refund in March silently moved a February sale into March, and the same report
run twice over the same closed period returned different numbers. An integration
test now records a refund against a closed period and asserts the figures do not
change.

**Provider money and wallet credit are never added.** They are separate columns
in every table on the screen. A wallet balance was already counted as revenue
when it was funded, so adding the two counts it twice — which is exactly the
mistake a single "revenue" figure invites.

**Refunds are reported on the day they were issued**, not against the sale they
reverse. "What did we refund in March" is a question an operator can act on;
"what did we sell in February that was later refunded" retroactively changes a
month that had closed.

## What the screen shows

| Section | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Trial conversion | Of the trials claimed in this period, how many of those customers have since paid for anything. |
| By kind of sale | New purchases, automatic renewals, extensions, upgrades, downgrades, add-ons, wallet top-ups, gifts, and digital goods, per currency. |
| By plan | Which plan version sold, at which billing period, and what it was worth at list price. |
| Provider money by day | One chart per currency, with the same figures as a table beside it. |
| Refunds issued | What went back, per currency, in this period. |

<Note>
  Trial conversion is a **cohort** measure and the screen says so beside the
  figure. The denominator is trials claimed inside the period; the numerator
  counts conversions at any later time. A period ending today therefore reads
  low — a trial claimed yesterday has had one day to convert — and that is a
  property of the question rather than a fault in the measurement.
</Note>

## The period

Four presets cover what people actually ask for — 7, 30, 90, and 365 days — and
the two date fields take anything else. A period longer than two years is
refused with the limit named, rather than becoming a query that runs until a
proxy times out.

**Days are bucketed in your own timezone**, taken from the browser and passed to
the API. "Sales on the third" means the operator's third; a report bucketed in
UTC puts an evening sale several hours east on the wrong day, and an integration
test asserts the same sale lands on the 2nd in UTC and the 3rd in Moscow.

The plan breakdown reads the code, version, and billing period off the plan
version each order actually referenced rather than off the plan's current state.
That is what plan versions are for: a price change last week must not re-price a
sale from last month.

## The export

`GET /v1/panel/reports/sales/export` takes the same four parameters and returns
one CSV with a `section` column — `operation`, `plan`, `day`, `refund`, `trial` —
rather than four files, because the breakdowns are four views of one period.

Amounts are exported in **minor units, unscaled**, exactly as they are stored.
Dividing by a hundred is wrong for every zero-decimal currency, and it is
something the reader can do knowing their own.

## Permissions

Both routes need `finance.read`, the same permission the order and payment
screens need. A sales report is money, and an installation that has decided who
may read money has already made this decision; a permission of its own would let
the two drift into disagreeing about the same figures.

## What is not here

Payment health per provider — the share of intents that settle, by adapter — is
a separate absence tracked in the roadmap. So is advertising attribution and
per-node traffic. This screen reports what was sold, not how well each acquirer
took the money or where the customer came from.
