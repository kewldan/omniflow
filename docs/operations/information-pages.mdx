title: Information pages
description: Publishing the FAQ, terms, offer, and privacy policy at addresses of your own, and why the body is never HTML
---

`/admin/content` publishes documents at addresses on your own installation:
`/pages/terms`, `/pages/privacy`, `/pages/offer`, `/pages/faq`, and anything else
you need.

Before this, the only content surface was news, and terms existed as a
`termsUrl` in the branding section pointing somewhere else entirely — so an
operator had to host and translate those documents outside the product. Payment
providers and application stores routinely require an offer and a privacy policy
at a stable address before they approve an account.

## Why these are not news posts

The difference is not cosmetic, and it decides the whole shape.

| News post | Information page |
| --- | --- |
| Dated, and expires | Permanent; content changes in place |
| Read once, counts towards an unread badge | Read whenever somebody needs it |
| For customers who signed in | For anybody, including a provider's reviewer |
| Identified by a generated id | Identified by its **address** |

That last row is the load-bearing one. A payment provider approves a URL, so the
slug is the page's identity in the database rather than a field beside a
generated identifier — there is no way to change an address while keeping the
page, because that would break an approval silently.

## Publishing, withdrawing, deleting

Three states, and the middle one is the one to reach for.

- **Draft** — no publication instant. The address answers 404 and the page is
  visible only in the panel.
- **Published** — readable by anybody at `/pages/{slug}`.
- **Unlisted but published** — a stable address with no menu entry, which is
  what a document that exists to satisfy a review needs.

**Withdrawing is reversible** and takes the address out of the world without
destroying it. **Deleting takes the address itself**, which is what a provider
approved, so it requires typing the slug to confirm.

A page needs at least one language, and a language needs both a title and a
body. Half a translation renders a heading over nothing, which is worse than an
absent language — so it is refused rather than stored.

**The language falls back rather than 404ing.** A page that exists in English
only still answers at its address for a Russian reader, because the address is
what somebody bookmarked and what a provider approved.

## The body is never HTML

This is the part worth reading before writing a page.

These documents are written by an operator and served from the origin that holds
the session cookie. Rendering operator text as HTML — with or without a
sanitiser — puts the whole page one sanitiser bug away from stored cross-site
scripting.

So the text is **parsed by the API into typed blocks and typed inline runs**, and
the browser renders text nodes and anchors from that structure. There is no HTML
in the path, which means there is nothing for a sanitiser to get wrong. The API
returns the parsed document; it never returns the source text to a reader.

The whole syntax:

| Written | Renders as |
| --- | --- |
| `## Heading` | A heading |
| `- item` | A bullet |
| `1. item` | A numbered item |
| A blank line | The end of a block |
| `[text](https://…)` | A link, **https only** |

Everything else is a paragraph, and consecutive lines join into one — a document
written with a hard wrap does not render as a line per source line.

There is no bold, no italic, no image, and no raw HTML, because a legal document
does not need them and each one is another construct to get wrong.

<Note>
  **An unrecognised construct becomes ordinary text.** A link with an `http://`
  or `javascript:` target renders as the literal characters you typed, brackets
  and all: visible, obviously wrong, and harmless. Parsing never refuses a
  document — an operator with a published page they cannot see and no line
  number is worse off than one with a paragraph that reads oddly.
</Note>

## The reader's side

`/pages` lists the published, listed documents; `/pages/{slug}` is one of them.
Both are **server-rendered**, because the readers who matter most — a payment
provider's reviewer, an application store's reviewer, a search engine — are not
guaranteed to run JavaScript. A terms page that needs hydration to appear is a
terms page that does not exist to the party requiring it.

The customer panel links to the list from the account screen, under the same
group as notification preferences and personal data.

`GET /v1/pages` and `GET /v1/pages/{slug}` are public and unauthenticated. They
carry nothing but what an operator published for everybody, and both are cached
for five minutes.

## Permissions and audit

Reading in the panel needs `marketing.read`; publishing, editing, and deleting
need `marketing.write` — the same permissions as news, because it is the same
capability: publishing words to customers. A support operator who may answer a
ticket has no business rewriting the terms of service.

Saves, publications, withdrawals, and deletions audit as `content.page.saved`,
`content.page.published`, `content.page.withdrawn`, and `content.page.deleted`
under the `marketing` category.
