title: Subscription, acquisition, and unit economics reporting
description: The paying base over time, recurring revenue and how it moved, renewals, churn and why customers leave, cohorts, what acquiring a customer cost and returned, unit economics, activation and usage, and support satisfaction
---

`/admin/reports` has four tabs beside sales, payment health, and channels. **Subscriptions** answers
how the paying base moves: how many hold paid access, what that is worth every month, how it moved,
how much of it renews and will renew, why customers leave, and what each month's new payers became.
**Acquisition** answers what bringing a customer in cost and returned, by channel and over time, and
how far new customers get before they pay. **Economics** answers what the period earned after every
cost Omniflow knows of. **Usage** answers whether customers use what they bought. All share the period
controls at the top of the screen with the other tabs.

The rules of the [sales report](/operations/sales-reporting) hold here too: provider money and wallet
credit are never added together, money is reported per currency and never converted, and a rate with
nothing behind it is shown as absent rather than 0%.

## The history this needs

Seven things are recorded that no other table keeps:

| Kept in | What | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `subscription_daily_snapshots` | The subscription base on each UTC day, by plan, billing period, kind, and currency, with its monthly recurring revenue | The worker's `subscription_snapshot` pass, hourly. It rewrites today's reading each time. On its first passes it reconstructs up to 365 past days from the entitlement history, about two months per pass |
| `node_traffic_samples` | Each Remnawave node's own traffic counter and online count, once an hour | The worker's `node_traffic_sample` pass. Samples older than 400 days are pruned |
| `funnel_events` | The first time a customer looked at the plans and opened a checkout, and on which channel | The bot and the customer panel, on the customer's own request; a failure to record never fails the request |
| `vpn_usage_observations` | Per Remnawave user: when they first connected, when they were last online, the node they last used, and their devices active in the last 30 days against their limit | The worker's `usage_census` pass, hourly, which pages through every panel user and device; and Remnawave's `user.first_connected` webhook, which records the moment exactly. The first connection is kept once seen, so a period whose panel user was later deleted still says whether it was used. An observation for reporting, like the node samples: it decides nothing |
| `operating_costs` | What running the service costs: servers, software, staff, other — each over a span of days, optionally against one node | An operator, by hand, under **Economics** |
| `payment_provider_fees` | What each payment provider keeps of a payment, from a date on | An operator, by hand, under **Economics** |
| `churn_feedback` | The reason a customer gave for leaving, from a closed list | The customer: in the bot when switching automatic renewal off or from the message that their subscription expired, and in the web cabinet when switching renewal off |

A reconstructed day is exact except where a refund or a pause has since moved an entitlement's end —
which is why the snapshot is kept rather than recomputed on every visit. Until the first passes have
run, the Subscriptions tab says so instead of showing empty charts.

## Subscriptions

### Kinds of access

Every active entitlement counts once per subscription, in one of four kinds:

| Kind | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Paid** | Bought here, with a price above zero after discount; wallet-funded counts, because the balance was money once |
| **Imported** | Carried over from the previous bot. It was paid for there and has no price here |
| **Trial** | A claimed trial |
| **Free** | Any other zero-value grant: a promo code's days, a campaign bonus, an operator's gift |

Refunded orders are out. Where an upgrade or an early renewal left two entitlements covering the
same moment, the one that started last is the one counted.

### Monthly recurring revenue

The price of each paid subscription, net of discount, as if it were billed every 30 days: a 90-day
plan sold for 900 contributes 300. Imported periods carry no price, so they are valued at the plan
version's **list price** in the same currency and shown apart as an estimate — a dashed line on the
chart, a separate figure on the card. After a migration most of the base is imported, and adding the
estimate to what was actually charged would present a guess as revenue.

### Renewals and churn

A paid or imported period that **ended** in the window is:

- **renewed** when another paid period for the same subscription started no later than **7 days**
  after its end (for a customer without subscriptions, any paid period of theirs);
- **pending** when it ended less than 7 days ago with no successor yet — the customer may still come
  back, and it stays out of the rate;
- **lapsed** otherwise.

The **renewal rate** is renewed ÷ (renewed + lapsed). **Churn** is lapsed periods over the paying base
(paid and imported) on the first day of the window. A customer who returns after the grace is not a
renewal; the sales report's [returning payers](/operations/sales-reporting) counts them.

### Automatic renewal

One row per funding source — wallet or saved card — over the billing cycles that started in the
window: succeeded on the first attempt, recovered by a retry, lost when the attempts ran out, or still
being retried, with the success rate over decided cycles. Below it, the most common failure codes, and
how many customers have automatic renewal on now.

### Movement of recurring revenue

Monthly recurring revenue per customer at the window's first moment and at its last (or now, for a
window that ends in the future), valued exactly as the snapshot values it. The difference is split
customer by customer:

| Line | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **New** | Nothing at the start, something at the end, and never paid before the window — here or in the previous bot |
| **Returning** | Nothing at the start, something at the end, and paid for something before the window |
| **Paying more** | More at the end than at the start |
| **Paying less** | Less at the end, but not nothing |
| **Lost** | Something at the start, nothing at the end |

**Net revenue retention** is (start + paying more − paying less − lost) ÷ start: what the customers
paying at the start pay at the end. Above 100% the growth among existing customers outweighs what was
lost. **Gross revenue retention** leaves the growth out. A customer who switches currency appears as
lost in one and new in the other: nothing is converted.

### Renewals ahead

The paid and imported periods that end in the next **30 days** and have no successor yet, by plan,
billing period, and currency, counted from today whatever the window: how many, how many will renew
themselves (automatic renewal is on), what renewing them all would bring at today's list price of the
same plan version, and what to expect — that value weighted by the plan's renewal rate over the last
**90 days**, or the overall rate when the plan has fewer than ten decided periods.

### Why customers leave

When a customer switches automatic renewal off — in the bot or the web cabinet — they are asked why,
from a closed list: too expensive, blocked on their network, too slow, no longer needed, switched to
another service, technical problems, other. The bot's message that a subscription expired carries a
**Why didn't you renew?** button that asks the same. Answering is optional and never blocks anything;
answering again the same day replaces the answer, and nothing a customer types is kept. The card shows
the answers given in the window by reason and occasion. They are the customers who chose to answer,
not every customer who left; read the shares, not the totals.

### Revenue per customer

Per currency: provider money taken in the window, the customers it came from, **ARPPU** (money per
paying customer), the average number of paying subscriptions per day of the window, and **ARPU**
(money per average subscription). Wallet spending is not provider money and is not in either.

### Cohorts

Customers by the month of their **first payment** — a provider payment here or one the previous bot
took — for the twelve months ending with the window's last month, whatever the window's start. Each
cell is the share of the cohort that held paid or imported access at some point in that month. Access
rather than payment, so a yearly subscriber reads as retained all year. **Lifetime value** is what the
cohort has paid so far per customer, per currency, net of refunds: a figure to date, not a forecast.

## Acquisition

### Recording costs

Omniflow sees the revenue an advertisement brought and never its invoice, so the cost is entered by
hand in **Advertising costs**: the day, the amount and currency, an optional note, and what it was
spent on — a [/start campaign](/operations/advertising-measurement) or a `utm_source` (stored in lower
case), never both. Recording and removing a cost needs `finance.write`; both are written to the audit
trail, and a retried request with the same `Idempotency-Key` records the cost once. Removing is for a
cost entered by mistake.

### Campaigns and sources

For each /start campaign: customers it brought in during the window, how many of them have paid since,
and per currency the revenue they brought against the cost recorded for the window. **CAC** divides the
cost by the paying customers; **ROI** is (revenue − cost) ÷ cost.

For each `utm_source`: paid orders in the window, their customers, how many paid for the first time,
revenue, and cost. CAC here divides by first-time payers. A source with a cost and no orders is listed —
it is the line most worth seeing.

CAC and ROI appear only where cost and revenue share a currency. Nothing is converted.

### Promo codes and what was given away

Each promo code redeemed in the window: redemptions, customers, how many were making a first purchase,
the discount given, and the provider money from the orders it was used on.

**Given away** lists wallet credit granted rather than bought, by its source (`promotion`, `referral`,
`referral_reward`, `wheel_spin`, `gift`, `operator_adjustment`, and the rest), and free access created in
the window by kind (`trial`, or the order operation that carried it) with the days it covered.

### Value by channel

The customers who paid for the **first time** in the window, by the channel that brought them: the
first touch Omniflow knows of, in this order — a /start campaign, an invitation, the `utm_source` of
their first paid order, otherwise organic. A customer is in one channel only, so the lines add up to
every new payer. Per channel and currency: new payers, **value to date** (what they have paid since,
net of refunds, per payer), revenue per payer per month since their first payment, **CAC** (the cost
recorded for that campaign or source in the window, or the referral rewards granted in it, per new
payer), **LTV : CAC**, and **payback** — the months of revenue that repay one payer's cost. Value is to
date, not a forecast, so a recent window reads low and its payback long.

### The referral programme

Customers invited in the window, how many qualified, how many were rejected, and how many have paid
since; the rewards granted in the window by role (to the inviter, to the invited, commission) as wallet
credit or days of access, with reversed rewards left out; and per currency, the money rewards against
what the invited customers paid. A reward is a cost when it is credited — withdrawing it later only
moves what was earned.

### By language

Customers who signed up in the window, by their interface language: how many claimed a trial and how
many paid.

### The purchase funnel

Of the customers who signed up in the window: how many looked at the plans, opened a checkout, claimed
a trial, and paid for a subscription — each step counted once per customer, whenever it happened. Below
it, the two recorded steps by the channel they were first reached on (`bot` or `web`). Like trial
conversion, it is a cohort figure and reads low for a window that ends today.

## Unit economics

**Economics** sets what the window earned against what it cost, per currency:

| Line | From |
| --- | --- |
| Provider money | Settled orders in the window, as on the sales tab |
| Refunds issued | Refunds on the day they were issued |
| Provider fees | Each payment charged the rate entered for its provider on the day it was paid |
| Net revenue | The three above |
| Running costs | The costs entered, spread evenly over the days each covers |
| Advertising | The advertising costs recorded for days in the window |
| Referral rewards | Money the referral programme credited in the window, not reversed |
| Contribution | What is left, with its **margin** over the money kept after refunds |

Beside it: the average number of paid and imported subscriptions over the window, and net revenue and
running costs per subscription per thirty days.

**Provider rates** are entered because providers do not report their fee in a common shape: a share in
basis points (350 is 3.5%) and an optional fixed amount per payment in one currency, from a date on. A
rate applies until a later one, so entering a new rate never changes an earlier month; a rate for a
date that already has one replaces it. A payment whose provider has no rate for its day is counted in
**without a rate** and its fee is left out rather than taken as zero — the card then says the fees are
an estimate.

**Running costs** are entered over a span of days — a monthly server bill as one cost from the 1st to
the 30th — and a report reads only the share that falls in its window. A cost tied to a node prices
that node's traffic: per node, the traffic it carried in the window from the hourly samples, the paying
customers whose last connection went through it, its cost, and the cost per GiB. The server costs over
all traffic give the service's own cost per GiB.

Entering and removing costs and rates needs `finance.write`; each change is audited, and a retried
cost with the same `Idempotency-Key` is recorded once.

## Usage and activation

**Usage** reads the census above. Until its first pass it says so instead of showing figures.

- **Activation of trials.** Of the trials claimed in the window: how many customers connected, how
  many within a day, the median time from claim to first connection, and the conversion to a payment
  of those who connected against those who never did. A cohort figure, low for a window ending today.
- **Activation of purchases.** Of the customers who paid for the first time in the window, how many
  connected, and how many within a day of paying.
- **Paying customers now.** Customers holding a paid or imported period today: how many the census has
  seen, online in the last 7 and 30 days, never connected, and at or over their device limit. A
  customer at the limit is the natural audience for more devices; one who never connected is worth a
  message before renewal.
- **By node.** Customers by the node they last connected through: paying customers, the paid periods
  of those customers that ended in the window and how many renewed, and the support tickets they
  opened, per hundred paying customers. A node is where a customer was last seen, so this points at a
  node worth a look rather than passing judgement on it.

A customer's connection is the earliest across every Remnawave user they hold, and they are online
when any of them was.

## Traffic history

`/admin/traffic` → **Traffic history** shows traffic per day across all nodes and, per node, the total
over 7, 30, or 90 days, the daily average, the busiest hour's online count, and the node's limit. It
reads the hourly samples above. A node's counter is cumulative within its cycle. A drop to less than
half the previous reading is a reset, and the new reading is taken as the traffic since it; a smaller
drop is the counter wobbling and counts as nothing. The live tables
above it are unchanged and still read Remnawave on every request. See
[Traffic reporting](/operations/traffic-reporting) and [decision 0016](/architecture/decisions#traffic-history-is-an-observation).

## Support satisfaction

Ten minutes after a ticket is resolved, and within three days of it, the bot asks its customer once for
a mark from 1 to 5. A tap records the mark on the ticket; a second tap, or a tap on someone else's
ticket, changes nothing. The **Support** desk's report shows, over the same 7, 30, or 90 days as the
workload figures: **CSAT** (the share of fours and fives), the average mark, how many marks arrived, the
response rate (marks over questions sent in the window), the distribution, and the same per operator the
ticket was assigned to. Customers the bot cannot reach are not asked in Telegram. The web cabinet shows
the same question under a resolved or closed conversation (`POST /v1/account/support/tickets/{ticketID}/rating`);
the first answer, from either surface, is the mark, and a mark given in the browser counts as asked, so the
bot does not ask again and the response rate never exceeds what was asked.

## Routes and permissions

| Route | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/subscriptions` | `finance.read` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/acquisition` | `finance.read` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/acquisition/spend` | `finance.read` |
| `POST /v1/panel/reports/acquisition/spend` | `finance.write` |
| `DELETE /v1/panel/reports/acquisition/spend/{spendID}` | `finance.write` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/economics` | `finance.read` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/usage` | `finance.read` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/costs` | `finance.read` |
| `POST /v1/panel/reports/costs` | `finance.write` |
| `DELETE /v1/panel/reports/costs/{costID}` | `finance.write` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/provider-fees` | `finance.read` |
| `POST /v1/panel/reports/provider-fees` | `finance.write` |
| `DELETE /v1/panel/reports/provider-fees/{feeID}` | `finance.write` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/traffic/history` | `customers.read` |
| `GET /v1/panel/reports/support-satisfaction` | `support.read` |

The period parameters are the sales report's: `since`, `until`, and `timezone`, at most two years.
The customer answers why they are leaving through `POST /v1/account/churn-feedback` with their own
session; the bot writes the same rows.
