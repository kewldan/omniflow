---
title: Mandatory channel subscription
description: Requiring Telegram channel membership, and the rules that stop it punishing people unfairly.
---

An operator may require customers to be members of one or more Telegram
channels. It is a marketing mechanism, and it is the only thing in Omniflow that
can suspend a paying customer for a reason unrelated to payment. Every part of
it is therefore reversible, warned about first, and auditable afterwards.

## An unknown answer is never absence

`getChatMember` can fail: Telegram is unreachable, the bot lost its admin rights
in the channel, the API is rate-limiting. None of those mean the customer left.

A failed check records `unknown`, which is a distinct state from `absent` and
blocks nothing. Folding the two together would suspend people because of an
outage, and that is the failure mode this mechanism has that nothing else in the
product does.

A customer with no membership record at all is in the same position: nobody has
established they are absent, so nothing is gated.

## Two requirements, two promises

| Flag | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `require_for_purchase` | Asks somebody to join before they pay |
| `require_for_activation` | Takes access away from somebody who already did |

They are separate because they are different promises. Only the activation
requirement can suspend; gating a purchase never takes anything away from
anybody.

The purchase requirement is applied on both surfaces, by the same rule. The bot
checks it when the customer presses **Pay**, asking Telegram live when its
cached answer is older than five minutes. The web checkout checks it when the
customer presses **Confirm**, but the API process has no Telegram transport of
its own, so it reads only the memberships the worker has recorded: an answer
older than twenty-four hours, or no answer at all, is *unknown*, and unknown
never blocks. A customer the web refuses is shown the channels with a join
button for each; once they join, the worker's next sweep — or **I joined** in
the bot — records the new state and the confirmation goes through. A customer
with no Telegram identity cannot be in a Telegram channel and is not asked.

## Leaving is warned about before it costs anything

The sequence is compliant → warned → suspended, and it is driven by a grace
period the operator configures.

The first check that finds a customer absent warns them and starts the clock.
Nothing else happens until the clock runs out. The warning fires once per lapse
rather than on every check, because a daily reminder to rejoin a channel is how
a customer learns to ignore the bot.

Three days is the default because it spans a weekend, which is when somebody is
most likely to tidy up their channel list and least likely to notice the
consequence.

A zero grace period is a legitimate configuration and suspends at once — but it
still sends the warning, because a customer suspended without warning is one who
cannot tell why.

### What the customer receives, and what is done

Each change of state sends one message to the customer's chat. The warning names
every channel still missing, with a join button for each, and the moment access
stops unless they rejoin before it. The suspension says access is off and that
rejoining brings it back at the next check. The restoration says it is back.
Nothing is sent on a check that changes nothing.

Suspension and restoration are not written to Remnawave directly. The worker
enqueues a `disable` fulfillment operation for every entitlement still inside
its paid period, and an `enable` for every one Remnawave reports disabled when
the customer returns, under an idempotency key that names the entitlement and
the moment of the decision. A customer suspended for leaving a channel therefore
carries the same operation history as one an operator disabled by hand, and an
operator reading the fulfillment log sees `channel-gate` as the correlation. An
entitlement an operator paused is never touched: it is off by a decision a
rejoin must not undo. The enforcement state is recorded before the consequence
is attempted, so a consequence that could not be enqueued is retried by the next
pass rather than lost.

## Rejoining restores automatically

A customer who fixes the problem should not have to ask somebody to undo the
consequence. The next check that finds them a member restores access, whether
they were warned or already suspended.

`left_at` is stamped the first time absence is seen and cleared on return, so
the grace clock measures from when the customer actually left rather than from
when a sweep noticed.

## Exemptions

The rule is a marketing mechanism, and there are always people it should not
apply to: a paying customer who cannot join for their own reasons, somebody the
operator promised, a test account.

An exemption records who granted it and why, because one nobody can explain is
indistinguishable from a mistake. It may carry an expiry.

Exempting a suspended customer restores them at once — the rule no longer
applies, so neither should its consequence. Revoking an exemption returns the
customer to `compliant` rather than straight to `suspended`: the next check
decides, and it warns first like anybody else. Suspending on revocation would
take access away with no notice.

## Caching and rate limits

Membership answers are cached for a configurable window, six hours by default.
`getChatMember` is rate-limited against a third party, and re-asking on every
screen would make the bot slower for everybody in order to enforce a marketing
rule.

The worker re-verifies in bounded batches, so a large installation is re-checked
over many passes rather than in one burst that gets the bot throttled.

## Turning it off

Disabling every channel stops enforcement immediately. The worker reads the
enabled set first and returns without doing anything when it is empty, rather
than continuing until some configuration cache expires.

## Settings

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `channel_grace_seconds` | 259200 (3 days) | How long access continues after leaving |
| `channel_recheck_seconds` | 21600 (6 hours) | How long a membership answer is trusted |
