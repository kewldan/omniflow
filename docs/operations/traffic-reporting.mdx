---
title: Traffic reporting
description: Which node is filling up and who consumed the most, read live from Remnawave, and why nothing on this screen is stored
---

`/admin/traffic` answers two questions that the anomaly signals cannot. Anomaly
detection answers "who should somebody look at". This answers **which node is
filling up** and **who consumed the most**, and it exports both.

## Nothing here is stored

Remnawave is authoritative for traffic, nodes, and connections. There is no
table in this repository for a node, and no column anywhere for a byte a
customer used — the reconciler records status, expiry, traffic *limit*, and
device limit, and deliberately not consumption.

So both halves of this screen are read from the panel on every request, and
there is no history. Keeping one would be the first step towards Omniflow having
an opinion about traffic, which is the boundary
[decision 0004](/architecture/decision-0004-remnawave-boundary) exists to hold.

What Omniflow adds is the one thing it can: the join. A Remnawave user
identifier resolved to the customer who holds it, and a link to their profile.

## Nodes

Sorted by pressure — nodes with a limit first, by how close they are to it, then
the unlimited ones by absolute use. The question is "which node is filling up",
and the answer belongs at the top.

A node with **no limit reports no saturation figure** rather than 0%. It cannot
be filling up, and rendering zero would sort it among the empty ones when it
belongs off that list entirely.

<Warning>
  **A panel that does not expose a node listing says so.** `nodesReported` is
  false and the screen renders the reason — the route is unsupported, the panel
  could not be reached, or no Remnawave connection is configured.

  This distinction is the point. An empty node table and "we could not ask" look
  identical and mean opposite things, and a page of zeros for an installation
  whose nodes are fine is worse than an honest absence.

  Only the fields Omniflow renders are decoded, and a panel that omits one
  leaves a zero rather than failing the request. If your panel version shapes
  its node listing differently, this section degrades and the rest of the page
  is unaffected — confirm it against your own panel rather than assuming from
  this page.
</Warning>

## Heaviest users

The fifty largest consumers in the current cycle, with lifetime use beside the
cycle figure. Both, because "this month" and "ever" are different questions and
the second explains the first.

**The ranking says how far it looked.** Finding the heaviest users means
scanning every user, and the panel pages at five hundred; the report stops after
ten pages. When it did not reach the end, the screen says the ranking covers the
users it scanned out of the total the panel reports. A truncated ranking
presented as a complete one is worse than no ranking.

**A user Omniflow did not create reads as "not linked"**, not as an error.
Somebody provisioned it in the panel directly, or an import has not run, and
both are real states.

## The export

`GET /v1/panel/reports/traffic/export` returns one CSV with a `section` column —
`node` and `consumer`.

Byte counts are **unscaled**. A gigabyte is 1000³ to some readers and 1024³ to
others, and choosing one in an export makes the file wrong for the other half.
The screen renders binary units and labels them as such (GiB, not GB), because
the conversion it applies divides by 1024 and calling that a gigabyte would be
seven percent out at that scale — enough to stop an operator's arithmetic
reconciling against their host's invoice.

## Permissions

Both routes need `customers.read`. The half that matters names customers and
their consumption; the node figures are the less sensitive half and travel with
it.
