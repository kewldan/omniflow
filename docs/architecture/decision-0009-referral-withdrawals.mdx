---
title: "0009 — Referral withdrawals hold money at request time and are decided by a person"
description: Why a withdrawal request debits the wallet immediately, why payouts happen outside Omniflow, and why the risk score never decides on its own
---

**Status:** Accepted.

## Context

Bedolaga lets a partner withdraw referral earnings: a request with payout
details, a minimum, a cooldown, a laundering score, and an operator who
approves, rejects, and pays. Its implementation debits the balance only on
approval, so between request and approval the same money can be spent on a
subscription and the approval then fails, or is withdrawn twice through two
requests. Omniflow's wallet is a double-entry ledger with serializable money
transactions; a withdrawal has to fit that model.

## Decision

**A request holds its amount at once.** The request row and a wallet debit
(`ledger_transactions.type = debit`, `reference_type = referral_withdrawal`) are
written in one serializable transaction. Rejection or cancellation posts the
matching credit; approval and payment move no ledger money. The available
amount is the wallet's available balance capped by unreversed inviter rewards
minus earlier requests when only referral earnings may leave.

**The payout happens outside Omniflow.** No bank, card, or crypto transfer
adapter is added. The operator pays by their own means and marks the request
paid; the payout details are sealed with the data key and revealing them is
audited.

**The risk score is advice.** It is deterministic, its reasons are shown, and
it never approves or rejects automatically.

**Multi-level programmes, partner programmes, and referral contests are built
on top of this ledger model** (rewards are ledger credits; withdrawals are
ledger debits), never as a second balance.

## Consequences

- A customer sees their balance drop the moment they request a withdrawal.
- An operator who never pays an approved request leaves the money held; the
  queue's "approved" filter is the list of payouts owed.
- No automated payout means no payout provider credentials to protect, and no
  way to pay out faster than a person can.

## Revisit when

An installation needs automated payouts through a provider API, which brings
payout credentials, provider idempotency, and reconciliation of a transfer that
may fail after the ledger says it succeeded.
