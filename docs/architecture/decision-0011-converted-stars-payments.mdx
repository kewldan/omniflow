---
title: "0011 — Converted Stars payments keep the order's currency and fix a quote per order"
description: Why a ruble order paid in Telegram Stars is recorded in rubles, how the Star count is fixed, and why exchange rates never silently fall back to a guess
---

**Status:** Accepted.

## Context

Bedolaga lets Telegram Stars pay ruble prices at an operator rate, and reads
USD/RUB automatically. Omniflow's ledger, orders, reports, and refunds are all
in the order's currency, and Stars settle only in `XTR`. Converting the order
itself to Stars would split one customer's history across currencies and make
every report, loyalty tier, and referral reward convert again.

## Decision

**The order and its payment intent stay in the order's currency.** The Star
count is a separate per-order quote (`stars_quotes`), fixed the first time it
is asked for and read by the invoice, the pre-checkout check, and settlement.
Settlement maps the Stars received back onto the order's currency through the
quote; anything but the exact quote is converted at the quote's rate and
classified like any provider's mismatch.

**Rates come from published sources with a bounded fallback.** USD/RUB: the
Central Bank of Russia, then open.er-api.com, cached for an hour, persisted;
a reading older than two days is not used, then the operator's manual value,
then "unavailable". An unavailable rate withdraws the Stars option for ruble
orders rather than guessing.

## Consequences

- Reports, refunds, and rewards need no conversion: the money is rubles.
- What Stars are worth to the operator after Telegram's fees is not modelled;
  the rate is the operator's business decision.
- An order quoted before a rate change keeps its old Star price until it
  expires.

## Revisit when

Another provider needs to settle in a currency other than the order's, at
which point the quote becomes a general conversion record on the payment
intent rather than a Stars-only table.
