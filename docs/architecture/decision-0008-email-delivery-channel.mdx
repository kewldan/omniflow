---
title: "0008 — E-mail is a queued delivery channel, not a credential"
description: Why outgoing e-mail goes through a sealed PostgreSQL queue with the Telegram consent policy, which transports are supported, and what this does not change about sign-in
---

**Status:** Accepted.

## Context

Bedolaga mails receipts, broadcasts, personal offers, and guest purchases, and
operators moving from it expect the same. Omniflow had one synchronous Resend
call for the sign-in link and nothing else. [Decision 0007](/architecture/decision-0007-no-customer-password)
recorded "there is no e-mail transport" as one reason customers have no
password; adding a transport reopens the question unless this record closes it.

## Decision

**E-mail is a delivery channel.** It carries receipts, account notices,
marketing the customer agreed to, and the one-time sign-in link. It is never a
place a secret the customer chose is reset to, and there is still no customer
password: the remaining reasons in 0007 — the panel must not answer "is this
address a customer here", and every customer arrives holding a stronger
credential — stand on their own.

**Everything except the sign-in link is queued in PostgreSQL** (`email_outbox`),
written in the transaction that decides to send it, keyed for deduplication, and
delivered by a worker periodic pass with bounded exponential retries. No broker,
consistent with [0001](/architecture/decision-0001-modular-monolith) and
[0002](/architecture/decision-0002-postgresql-only-durability). The rendered
message is sealed with the data key.

**Marketing e-mail uses the Telegram policy**, not a second one: the same
`commerce.EvaluateDelivery`, the same suppression list, one frequency cap
counted across both channels, and consent held on the verified address. Every
marketing message carries an RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe that needs no
session.

**Two transports: Resend and plain SMTP.** SMTP covers every other provider and
self-hosted relays; provider-specific APIs beyond Resend are not added.

## Consequences

- An address is reachable only once verified by an e-mail sign-in, so an
  installation that never enables e-mail sign-in has no e-mail audience.
- Quiet hours do not apply to e-mail: it is not a push notification.
- Delivery status stops at "accepted by the transport". Bounces and complaints
  are not ingested; an operator suppresses a complaining customer by hand.
- Sealing the payload means the panel can show counts and reasons, never the
  content of a queued message.

## Revisit when

An installation needs bounce or complaint feedback to keep its sender
reputation, which requires a webhook from the transport; or a customer-chosen
secret is proposed for any purpose, which reopens 0007 rather than this record.
