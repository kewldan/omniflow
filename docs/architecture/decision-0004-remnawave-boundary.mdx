---
title: "0004 — Remnawave owns VPN state; Omniflow records intent"
description: Why entitlement is stored separately from observed Remnawave state, why nothing writes to Remnawave storage, and what that costs
---

**Status:** Accepted.

## Context

Omniflow sells access that Remnawave delivers. Two systems therefore hold
overlapping-looking facts about the same customer: Omniflow knows what was paid
for, and Remnawave knows what is currently true of the VPN user — traffic used,
devices connected, expiry, squad membership, whether an operator edited it by
hand this morning.

There are two ways to make that one system. Omniflow could treat Remnawave's
database as its own and write to it directly, which is fast, or it could mirror
Remnawave's state into its own tables and treat the mirror as truth, which is
tidy. Both fail the same way: the moment somebody changes something in the
Remnawave panel, the two systems disagree and nothing in the design says which
one is wrong.

## Decision

Remnawave is authoritative for VPN users, traffic, devices, access links, nodes,
hosts, squads, and Xray state. Omniflow never writes to Remnawave storage, never
manages Xray, and reaches the panel only through the versioned adapter over its
supported API.

Omniflow stores an **entitlement**: what a paid order entitles a customer to.
That is a separate record from observed Remnawave state, and the two are joined
by exactly one link. When they disagree, the disagreement is recorded as drift
with a reason an operator can read, rather than silently resolved in either
direction.

Every write is a fulfillment operation: idempotent, retried with backoff when
Remnawave is unavailable, and correlated to the order that justified it.

## Consequences

Money and access fail independently, which is the property the whole design
exists to produce. A successful payment is never lost because Remnawave is down;
it commits, and the fulfillment job keeps trying. An operator editing a user in
the Remnawave panel produces drift for review rather than a silent overwrite on
the next sync.

The cost is a whole reconciliation surface that a single-database design would
not need: drift detection, mismatch reasons, an operator view, and safe handling
of Remnawave users that were edited or deleted outside Omniflow. That machinery
is not incidental complexity — it is the visible form of the fact that two
systems own different halves of one product.

It also means Omniflow cannot answer some questions without asking Remnawave.
Device lists and live traffic are fetched, not stored, which is why they are
subject to Remnawave's availability and why the customer surfaces have explicit
stale and unavailable states.

Compatibility is bounded to what the contract tests actually exercise, currently
the Remnawave 3.2.x API surface. A version outside that is unsupported rather
than untested-but-probably-fine.

## Revisit when

- Remnawave's supported API stops offering an operation Omniflow needs, and the
  only alternatives are writing to its storage or dropping the capability. The
  correct outcome is still not to write to its storage.
- A future Remnawave major version changes the ownership split itself — for
  example by owning billing — at which point this record is superseded rather
  than amended.
