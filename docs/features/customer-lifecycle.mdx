title: Customer lifecycle
description: How customers arrive, how you look after them from the customer card, and how they are blocked, go quiet, are messaged, and leave
---

Every person who presses `/start` or signs in to the web cabinet becomes one customer record. That
record owns their subscriptions, wallet, orders, support conversations, consent, and contact
channels, whichever surface they used. This page follows a customer through their life at your
installation: how they get in, what you can see and do from the customer card, how you block
somebody, what happens to customers who stop showing up, how you talk to them, and what happens when
they leave.

The detailed references are [Admin operations](/operations/admin-operations),
[Notifications and announcements](/customer/notifications), and
[Personal data and privacy](/customer/privacy).

## Where customers come from

Most customers create themselves: the first `/start` in the bot or the first sign-in on the web
creates the record, under a lock that stops two simultaneous first contacts from making two accounts.
If you already have a customer base, two tools bring it in.

| Source | Tool | What it produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Users already in Remnawave | The **Imports** card on `/admin/customers` | A customer and a Remnawave mapping per user, so the bot recognises them by Telegram ID on their next `/start` |
| A CSV exported from another panel | **Upload CSV** on the same card | The same, from a file with `remnawave_id` and optionally `telegram_id`, `username`, `status` (up to 50,000 rows) |
| A Bedolaga bot | `cmd/migrate-bedolaga` | Customers, balances, subscriptions, and referrals, plus the history segments need — spent trials, past subscriptions, and payments — with a rehearsed cutover that keeps every subscription link working |

The Remnawave import runs in two phases: **Preview** stages every record and counts the clean ones,
the conflicts, and the unreadable ones before anything is written; **Apply** then commits the clean
ones. A record whose Telegram ID already belongs to a customer is left as a conflict rather than
merged. `APP_REMNAWAVE_AUTO_IMPORT_HOURS` makes the worker repeat the whole import on a schedule, so a
user you create directly in Remnawave becomes a customer without anybody pressing a button.

<Note>
  An import does not invent subscriptions, orders, or payments. Remnawave does not record what a
  customer paid, so the imported customer keeps their live access in Remnawave and Omniflow starts
  tracking money from their first purchase. A Bedolaga migration is different: it carries balances
  and subscriptions over, and keeps past trials, subscriptions, and payments as history that
  segments, loyalty, and reports read, so customers who paid and left before the move can still be
  found. See [Migrating from Bedolaga](/operations/migrate-from-bedolaga#history-trials-periods-and-payments).
</Note>

## The customer card

`/admin/customers` finds a customer by their Omniflow ID, Telegram ID, or Remnawave username. There
is deliberately no free-text search over names or addresses, because contact details are stored
encrypted precisely so they cannot be trawled. **Filter and sort** narrows the list instead: access
expiring within N days, a positive balance, auto-renew on, a restriction, trial-only customers,
customers from a campaign, recent activity, or heavy traffic use.

Opening a customer shows:

- **The header.** Their Telegram profile photo, fetched from the Bot API when the card opens and
  never stored, or two letters of their ID when there is none; and a copy button for the ID.
- **The overview.** Status, Telegram ID, language, and counts of subscriptions, orders, open tickets,
  and referrals.
- **Badges.** **N flagged for review** when risk controls matched something, **Allowlisted**, and
  **Dormant since …** for a customer who has gone quiet (see below).
- **Tabs.** Subscriptions, orders, wallet, referrals and loyalty, support, notifications, controls,
  activity, risk, and the audit timeline. Each tab appears only for operators whose role holds its
  permission — the wallet needs `finance.read`, risk needs `risk.read`, the timeline `audit.read`.

The **Activity** tab is the customer's own action log in the bot and the cabinet — which screen,
button, or command, and when — with route names only, never anything they typed. It is kept for
ninety days. The **Notifications** tab shows what was sent to the customer and, when something was
not, which rule held it back.

### What you can do from the card

| Action | Where | Permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add days, give the trial, or change the plan for the time left | Controls | `subscriptions.write` |
| Buy a plan with the customer's own balance | Controls | `finance.write` |
| Adjust the wallet | Wallet | `finance.write` |
| Stop wallet top-ups, purchases, or support for this customer, with a reason | Controls | `customers.write` |
| Turn auto-renew off, or back on where the customer consented before | Controls | `subscriptions.write` |
| Reset the trial so the customer may take it again | Controls | `subscriptions.write` |
| Write to the customer in Telegram in your own words | Support | `support.write` |
| Suspend or reactivate the account, with a reason | Header | `customers.write` |
| Mark for deletion, or anonymise | Controls, after typing a confirmation word | `customers.write` |
| Merge this account into another | Below the tabs | `customers.write` |

Every action is written to the audit trail. A merge is for the customer who bought in Telegram and
later signed in on the web with a provider the first account never had: the account you are looking
at is the one being absorbed, and a preview shows what will move before anything does.

## Blocking and unblocking

There are three strengths of block, and the lightest one that does the job is usually right.

1. **A restriction** stops one thing — wallet top-ups, purchases, or support — and leaves everything
   else working. The customer sees that the action is not available and a support button.
2. **Suspension** puts the whole account on hold. In the bot every screen is replaced by "Account
   suspended" with a support button; in the cabinet every request is refused and live sessions end.
   Support keeps working, because you usually suspend somebody in order to talk to them.
   **Reactivate** reverses it. Both need a reason.
3. **Moderation bans** on `/admin/customers` suspend a pasted list of up to 1,000 customer or
   Telegram IDs at once, optionally sending a ban message from a template (torrents, account sharing,
   spam, payment fraud, rules) or your own text.

<Note>
  Suspension does not write to Remnawave directly. The fulfillment pipeline observes customer state,
  because a panel click that bypassed it would leave Omniflow and Remnawave disagreeing with no record
  of why.
</Note>

The same Moderation card counts customers who blocked the bot (**Scan now**) and can suspend the
blockers who hold no live subscription; a customer with running access is never touched. With
`disableBlockedSubscriptions` in the marketing section, live subscriptions of a customer who blocked
the bot for longer than `blockedGraceHours` (24 by default) are disabled, and enabled again as soon as
the bot hears from them. The moderation section's **Filter dangerous display names** refuses the bot
to accounts whose name pretends to be staff or carries a link.

## Customers who go quiet

The `lifecycle` section on `/admin/settings/integrations` handles customers who stopped using the
service. It is off by default.

| Field | Default | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dormancyEnabled` | off | Turns the pass on |
| `inactiveDays` | 180 | Days without any activity before a customer is marked dormant |
| `deleteAfterDays` | 0 | Days of dormancy before the account is deleted. Zero never deletes anybody |

A customer is marked dormant only when they have done nothing for `inactiveDays` — no sign-in, no
message or button in the bot — and have nothing to lose: no live or limited subscription, nothing in
the wallet, no open support ticket, no order waiting for payment. Dormancy is a flag, not a status.
The customer sees nothing, and the card shows **Dormant since …**.

Any activity clears the flag at once — a database trigger does it, so the bot, the cabinet, and the
Mini App all revive a customer without having to remember to. Deletion is a second, opt-in step: with
`deleteAfterDays` above zero, a customer who stayed dormant that long is deleted through the same
deletion flow an operator starts from the card, and the privacy and retention rules apply unchanged.
The pass is checked hourly and runs at most once a day.

## Deletion and privacy

A customer manages their own data on `/account/privacy` in the cabinet: account state, contact
channels, consent history, a personal-data export, and a deletion request.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The customer asks">
    They write a reason and confirm. This needs a recent sign-in, and it records a request — it does
    not delete anything. They can cancel it while it is pending.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You delete">
    From the card's Controls tab, after typing the confirmation word, you mark the account deleted.
    Sessions end and the bot shows "This account has been deleted".
  </Step>
  <Step title="You anonymise">
    Anonymisation runs only on a deleted account and cannot be undone. It revokes every identity,
    wipes contact channels, cuts the Telegram ID from the Remnawave mapping, replaces support message
    bodies with `[anonymized]`, and deletes the referral code.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  The financial and audit record survives anonymisation on purpose. An order that funded a ledger
  entry cannot be removed without unbalancing the ledger, so an anonymised customer keeps their
  financial history and loses everything that identifies who placed it.
</Warning>

## Notifications and preferences

Every message to a customer passes through one decision: its kind, the customer's per-kind switches,
their marketing consent, their quiet hours, the marketing frequency cap, and whether Telegram can
reach them. The answer is send now, wait until a stated time, or do not send and record why.

- **Transactional messages** — expiry and renewal reminders, traffic warnings, payment and
  activation results, support replies, announcements — need no consent. A support reply that cannot
  reach the customer in Telegram — no Telegram account, a blocked bot — is e-mailed to their verified
  address instead, with a link to the conversation in the web cabinet.
- **Marketing messages** — campaigns and `news` posts — need marketing consent, which is off until
  the customer turns it on, and are limited by `APP_MARKETING_FREQUENCY_CAP` (3 per
  `APP_MARKETING_WINDOW_HOURS`, 168 hours, by default) and, when you set it, by a second cap over a
  longer window, `APP_MARKETING_LONG_FREQUENCY_CAP` per `APP_MARKETING_LONG_WINDOW_HOURS` (720 hours).
  "At most one a week and two a month" is `1` per `168` plus `2` per `720`.
- **Quiet hours** hold non-urgent messages until the window ends, in the customer's own time zone.
  Payment, activation, support, and incident messages always arrive.

Customers set all of this in the bot's `/settings` or on `/account/preferences`: switches for expiry,
traffic, renewal, and news, marketing consent, a quiet window, and a time zone. Every consent change
is appended as a record with its date, surface, and policy version, never edited. Unsubscribing stops
marketing and news but never expiry, traffic, or renewal reminders, because those carry facts a
customer needs to keep their access. To change the wording of the automatic notices, see
[Message wording](/operations/message-wording).

## News and broadcasts

Two tools reach many customers at once, and they serve different purposes.

| Tool | Where | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News posts | `/admin/content` | Announcements that stay readable in the bot's and the cabinet's news inbox, with a push when published. Draft, schedule, unpublish, archive |
| Campaigns | `/admin/marketing` | One template sent to one audience segment, by Telegram or e-mail, with an audience estimate before scheduling and a test send to your operator group |

Each news category maps to one of four kinds: `news`, `announcement`, `incident`, or `maintenance`.

<Warning>
  A post in a `news` category is marketing: it needs consent and spends the frequency cap. Publish an
  outage notice as an `incident` or an `announcement`, or only customers who opted into offers will
  see it pushed.
</Warning>

## Account-sharing signal

When an operator opens a subscription's devices on the customer card, the list carries a sharing
signal computed from what Remnawave reports about those devices: five or more devices active within a
day, four or more platforms, or three or more models of one platform. One reason reads as **Possibly
shared**, two or more as **Likely shared**.

The signal is read-only by design. Omniflow stores no device history and blocks, removes, or messages
nobody because of it — Remnawave owns devices and their limits. What you do with it is your decision:
talk to the customer, or use the account-sharing ban template.

## Retention

The worker's hourly retention sweep removes disposable operational data: expired bot and checkout
sessions, processed payment webhooks after 30 days, support attachments after 90 days, outbox events,
and resolved drift records. It never touches financial, audit, consent, identity, or entitlement
history, which is kept for the life of the installation. Retention runs only in the `worker`
process; an installation without one accumulates everything.

<Note>
  Customer sessions, sign-in links, and customer security events have documented windows (30 and 180
  days) that no job enforces today. Expired sessions still cannot be used, but the rows remain. See
  [Privacy](/operations/privacy).
</Note>
