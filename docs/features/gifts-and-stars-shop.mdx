Two features turn the bot into more than a place to renew your own subscription. **Gifts** let
a customer pay for VPN access, an add-on, or wallet credit and hand it to somebody else — a
friend, a relative, a person who has never opened your bot. The **digital goods shop** sells
Telegram Stars and Telegram Premium from the same bot, with the same wallet and payment
providers, so a customer who came for Stars can leave with a VPN trial.

Both are ordinary orders. Wallet balance, promo codes, payment providers, refunds, and order
history work exactly as they do for a plan, and both appear in your sales reports. This page
explains what each does and how to switch it on; the full mechanics are on
[Gifts and digital goods](/commerce/gifts-and-goods).

## Gifts

### What the customer sees

In the bot a customer opens **Gifts**, chooses a subscription or an amount of wallet credit,
and is asked *Who is it for?* They can send a Telegram `@username`, an e-mail address, or tap
**Just a code**. An optional note follows, then payment. On the web the same purchase is on
`/account/gifts`, with an optional **Recipient** field.

Whatever they choose, the sender receives a **claim code** once, on that screen. It is sixteen
characters with no easily confused letters. Only a hash of it is stored, so a lost code cannot
be recovered — not by you, and not by anybody reading the database or a backup.

### How the gift reaches the recipient

| Addressed to | What happens after payment |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody (just a code) | The sender passes the code on however they like |
| A `@username` already in the bot | The bot claims it for them within a minute and tells them what they got, with the note |
| A `@username` not in the bot yet | The gift waits; the first time that person writes to the bot, it is claimed and announced |
| An e-mail address | A separate claim code and activation link are mailed to that address |

A recipient can always redeem a code by hand: in the bot, or on the web wallet page in the
**Promo or gift code** field. A gifted subscription **extends** whatever the recipient already
has rather than replacing it, and the sender is told once when the gift arrives.

The gift for somebody not in the bot yet is worth highlighting to customers: it is how a
customer can bring a friend to your service without the friend doing anything first. The sender
is told at once that the person has not used the bot and until when the gift will wait.

### Rules that protect you

- The order belongs to the sender and never appears in the recipient's payment history.
- A sender cannot redeem their own gift, so the gift path cannot be used to reach a promotion
  or reward the sender is not eligible for.
- After ten wrong attempts a code stops answering at all, and every refusal shows the same
  message, so codes cannot be guessed or probed.
- An unclaimed gift expires after its lifetime. You can then refund it to the sender.

### Managing gifts

`/admin/gifts` lists every gift with its status — awaiting payment, claimable, claimed, expired,
revoked, refunded — the last four characters of its code, the sender, and the expiry. Support can
use those four characters to confirm which gift a conversation is about.

You can **revoke** a gift that has not been claimed, with a reason. A claimed gift cannot be
revoked or refunded to the sender: the recipient already holds what it bought, and refunding it
would give the sender their money back while the recipient keeps the access.

<Note>
  E-mail recipients need `APP_DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, because the address is encrypted before it is
  stored. Without it the bot accepts only `@usernames`, and the web refuses e-mail recipients.
  The address is erased once the mail is queued.
</Note>

## Guest purchases

A visitor with no account can buy a subscription at `/guest` with only an e-mail address — for
themselves or as a gift to another address. Link to `/guest` from your own website or landing
page.

It is offered when an [e-mail transport](/integrations/email) is configured and `APP_PUBLIC_URL`
is set. Every payment provider that settles your shop currency is available except Telegram Stars
and manual transfers, which need a chat or an operator. After payment the activation code is
e-mailed; the link opens the web wallet, where the visitor signs in with the same address and
redeems it. Throwaway mailboxes are refused, and one client address can start at most five
purchases an hour.

Guest orders belong to one system account, *guest checkout*, which appears in the customer list
and cannot sign in.

## The digital goods shop

The shop sells two things: **Telegram Premium** for 3, 6, or 12 months, and **Telegram Stars**,
either as fixed packs or as a custom amount the customer picks. Nothing in the shop touches
Remnawave, so a failed Stars delivery can never affect anybody's VPN access.

### Choosing how Stars and Premium are bought

Fragment has no public API, and buying there ends with a TON payment from a wallet. You choose
who holds that wallet.

| Provider | Sells | Who holds the Fragment session and TON wallet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gateway | Stars and Premium | A separate gateway service you run; Omniflow calls it over HTTP |
| `fragment_direct` | Stars only | Omniflow itself, from secrets in the environment |

**The gateway** keeps all spending power outside Omniflow. Configure it in `/admin/shop` →
**Providers**: its URL and token are encrypted with `APP_DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and never shown
again after you save them.

**Direct buying** suits operators who already run a Fragment account and TON wallet and do not
want a second service. Its three secrets are environment variables only:

| Variable | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `APP_FRAGMENT_COOKIES` | The session cookies of a KYC-verified fragment.com account |
| `APP_FRAGMENT_WALLET_MNEMONIC` | The 24-word seed phrase of the paying TON wallet |
| `APP_FRAGMENT_TON_API_KEY` | The key for toncenter or tonapi, where the wallet is read and the payment sent |

<Warning>
  These three secrets live in the environment of the api, bot, and worker and nowhere else. They
  are never written to the database, never returned by the API, never shown in the panel, and
  never logged; the panel can only say whether the provider is configured and which setting is
  missing. Keep them in `.env` or your secrets manager, never in a panel field, a ticket, or a
  repository. The seed phrase spends real money: keep only working capital in that wallet.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Try a dry run">
    Set `APP_FRAGMENT_DRY_RUN=true` and restart. Orders pass through every state without
    contacting Fragment or the wallet, and the secrets are not needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the secrets">
    Set the three variables above, turn the dry run off, and restart the api, bot, and worker.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch the provider on">
    `fragment_direct` is already listed under **Providers**, switched off. Enable it, then check
    the **Stars** tab: provider state, wallet address and balance, and how many Stars it covers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make one small real purchase">
    Buy the minimum of 50 Stars to yourself before opening the product to customers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The other `APP_FRAGMENT_*` settings — wallet version, TON API provider and rate limit, a proxy,
a fixed TON rate, the low-balance threshold — are on [Configuration](/getting-started/configuration).

### Products and pricing

`/admin/shop` → **Products** → **New product** creates Premium or a fixed Stars pack, hidden. Set
its price in the pricing editor, then make it visible. A custom-amount Stars product — minimum and
maximum, up to twelve preset buttons, a price per Star — is created in one step on the **Stars**
tab and adds **Buy Stars** to the bot's main menu.

A price is either:

- **A markup** over what the provider charges, followed by a rounding rule that always rounds up;
- **A fixed amount**, required for Premium, because no provider publishes Premium's cost;
- **A fixed price per Star**, charged whatever Fragment asks that minute.

A provider-quoted price expires after a short time and is re-taken rather than honoured late.
Promotions apply to plans *or* to shop products, never both, and a promo code that would push a
quoted price below the provider's cost is refused rather than quietly capped.

### Paying from the wallet

Wallet balance pays first, as for any order. When it does not cover the total, the bot shows the
shortfall with every payment method for exactly that amount. For a product with a fixed price the
purchase is saved and placed from the wallet automatically as soon as the top-up settles; if the
price rose in the meantime, nothing is charged and the customer is asked to confirm again.

After a delivery, a buyer without an active paid subscription is offered VPN — a trial if they
never had one. Turn that off with `APP_GOODS_VPN_OFFER_ENABLED=false`.

### Delivery and the review queue

Each order has exactly one delivery, so a retried job can never buy twice. A failure is sorted
into one of three outcomes:

- **Retried** — the provider is down, a session expired, or the wallet is short. After six
  attempts, a little over two hours, the customer is refunded to their wallet.
- **Refunded** — the username does not exist or the order was rejected outright.
- **Parked** — money may already have left, but nobody can tell. These go to
  **Needs review**.

A parked delivery is never retried or refunded automatically: retrying could pay twice, refunding
could return money for Stars that arrived. An operator with `goods.manage` checks the wallet's
outgoing transfers against the Fragment request shown on the order, then marks it delivered,
retries it, or refunds it, with a reason that goes to the audit log.

<Warning>
  The gateway does not honour an idempotency key and reports success even for a purchase it later
  abandoned. With the gateway, read "delivered" in the panel as "submitted and accepted", and
  expect more parked deliveries than with direct buying.
</Warning>

### Spend controls and monitoring

Each provider carries a **spend ceiling** over a rolling window, measured in what the provider
charges you rather than what customers pay, and a low-balance threshold. A zero ceiling means no
ceiling. The gateway publishes no balance, so its low-balance alert cannot fire; direct buying
reads the wallet itself.

With direct buying every sale is posted to your operator chat with buyer, recipient, Stars,
price, cost in TON and in your currency, margin, and the remaining wallet balance — with a top-up
request when it covers fewer Stars than `APP_FRAGMENT_WALLET_LOW_STARS`. The **Stars** tab shows
revenue, cost, and margin for any period.

## Steam top-ups

The bot can also top up a customer's Steam wallet: they type their Steam login, pick an amount in
dollars, and pay from the wallet or with any payment method, exactly as for Stars. The money comes
from your prepaid balance with [PlayWallet](https://playwallet.bot/ru/api) or
[Nervixy](https://nervixy.digital/docs-api) — each product names the provider it is sold through,
so you can switch between them or keep one in reserve.

The feature is off until you turn it on. Each provider exists only while its key is in the
environment (`APP_PLAYWALLET_API_KEY`, `APP_NERVIXY_API_KEY`), and a configured provider starts
switched off with no product, so nothing reaches customers until you enable the provider, create a
Steam product, price it per dollar, and make it visible. Then `🎮 Top up Steam` appears in the
bot's main menu; the web shop does not sell Steam top-ups.

A top-up cannot be undone, so the bot restates the login above the button that pays, and Nervixy
checks that the account exists before anything is charged. A top-up the provider refuses is
refunded to the customer's wallet; one whose outcome is unknown is parked in **Needs review**
rather than retried or refunded. Setup, pricing, and delivery are on
[Gifts and digital goods](/commerce/gifts-and-goods#steam-top-ups).
