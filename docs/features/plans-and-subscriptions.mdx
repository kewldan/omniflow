title: Plans and subscriptions

---

Everything a customer pays for in Omniflow is a **plan**, and everything they hold afterwards is a
**subscription**: one Remnawave user with an expiry, a traffic allowance, a device limit, and a set
of squads (servers). This page walks through what you can sell and how each capability is switched
on. It is a guide, not a reference: every section ends with a link to the page that has the exact
rules and refusals.

Two places hold almost all of it. The plan catalogue is at `/admin/catalog`. Installation-wide rules
— upgrades, downgrades, resets, trial guards, limited access — are at `/admin/settings/commerce` →
**Plan changes and resets**. A few limits are environment variables, read at start-up; they are
named where they apply.

## Plans and immutable versions

A plan is the product the customer sees: a code, a name and description in Russian and English, a
kind, a visibility switch, and a sort order. What it costs and what it gives live on a **plan
version**: duration, billing period, traffic, device limit, squads, grace period, upgrade and
downgrade policies, and one price per currency.

A version is never edited. To change a price or a limit, open the plan in `/admin/catalog`, press
**New version** (the editor is pre-filled from the current one), change what you need, type a reason,
and press **Publish version**. New purchases use the new version; every order already placed keeps
the version it was bought at, so publishing never changes what anyone already paid.

<Warning>
  Two things have no control in `/admin` and are done through the operator-token API: **creating a
  plan** (`POST /v1/admin/plans`, which creates the plan and its first version) and **writing its
  Russian and English copy**. A plan with no copy in a customer's language is not shown to that
  customer, and a plan with no price in `APP_DEFAULT_CURRENCY` is left out of the catalogue
  entirely. Check both first when a plan does not appear.
</Warning>

**Best deal** badges a plan in the bot and on the web store without changing its price. Hiding a
plan uses the **Visible** switch; archiving it stops new purchases without touching anyone who
already holds it. An installation that sells exactly one thing can turn on **Simple subscription**
on the catalogue page, which replaces the plan list in the bot and on the web with one offer and one
**Buy** button.

## Periods: months, custom days, and daily plans

A version has its own duration and price. Under **More periods and custom days** in the version
editor you can sell the same version for other periods — each row is a number of days, a price per
currency, and two flags: **Purchases** (offered on a first purchase or a plan change) and
**Renewals** (offered on an extension and on automatic renewal). **Custom days from / up to** with a
**Price per day** lets the customer type any number of days in that range.

When the version sells more than its own period, both the web checkout and the bot ask which one: the
bot shows a button per period with its price between the plan and the payment method, on a purchase
and on a renewal, and the payment methods then show the chosen period's price. Tick **Highlight** on
one period per currency to mark it as the recommended one — the bot labels it "best price".

A **daily plan** bills one day at a time from the wallet. Turn on **Bill daily from the balance** on
a version with the **Day** billing period, a one-day duration, and no other periods. The first day is
bought like any plan; after that the worker charges the wallet ten minutes before each day ends.
When the balance will not cover the next day, the customer is warned once, access stops with the
paid day, and a top-up within 30 days resumes it. Daily plans are the one case where the customer
can **Pause** and **Resume** billing themselves, on the web subscription page and in the bot.

## Traffic: fixed allowances, packages, and per-GB volumes

**How traffic is sold**, under **Traffic** in the version editor, is set per version:

| Mode | What the customer gets |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed | The version's own allowance. No traffic add-ons are sold for it |
| Fixed, with top-ups | The version's own allowance, which traffic add-ons can top up later. The default |
| Chosen at checkout | The customer picks a **package** you define, or a **custom volume** priced per gigabyte, at checkout |

A package is a volume in GB and what it adds to the price. A custom volume is bounded by **From, GB**
and **Up to, GB** and priced with a **Price per GB** per currency; both are priced for the version's
own duration and scaled for other periods. An empty traffic field on the version means unlimited.
**Traffic counter resets** passes Remnawave's own reset strategy (none, daily, weekly, monthly) to
the user.

## Device limits

Each version sets a device limit; empty means unlimited. Customers manage their connected devices on
the web dashboard (when they have one subscription) and `/account/devices`, and from the
subscription screen in the bot. On top of the plan's limit you can sell extra device slots as
add-ons (below), capped installation-wide by **Most devices per subscription** under **Plan changes
and resets**. **Remove devices on renewal** in the same card clears every registered device when a
subscription is renewed; it is off by default.

## Servers and choosing them at checkout

A version always assigns its own squads to every subscription, picked by name from the live
Remnawave list. It can also offer squads for the customer to choose: set **Squad selection** to
`optional` or `required`, set the minimum and maximum, and add a row per squad with a Russian and an
English label. The web checkout then shows a **Servers** fieldset, and the bot opens a squad
configurator after the payment method is chosen.

**Servers** on the catalogue page lets you describe and limit each Remnawave squad without managing
it — the nodes stay in Remnawave:

| Field | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Name in each language, Country | The label and flag customers see wherever they choose squads |
| Customer limit | The most customers a sale may put on the squad. A full squad is refused to new customers; renewals and customers already on it are never refused |
| Price for choosing it | What choosing the squad adds to the plan price, scaled to the period bought |
| Trials may use it | Whether a trial order keeps this squad |

A new version reaches new purchases only. **Move live subscriptions onto the newest squads** under a
plan's **Plan settings** pushes the newest version's squads to everyone already on the plan.

## Trials and trial abuse controls

A trial is a plan whose kind is `trial`, with a trial rule on each version: `new_customer` (the
default), `never_trialled`, or `any`. Each customer can claim one trial per installation, ever — a
second subscription never brings a second trial. The claim is written inside the order, so a double
tap cannot mint two.

The guards you control:

- `APP_TRIAL_MINIMUM_ACCOUNT_AGE_HOURS` (default `0`) refuses a trial to an account younger than this.
- **No trial for accounts that sign in only with** — Telegram, an e-mail link, or OpenID Connect —
  refuses customers whose every sign-in method is in the list.
- A trial is also refused while the customer has a running subscription, and when a new account
  reuses an identity that already used a trial.
- **Trials may use it** on each server limits which squads a trial reaches.

A refused trial is still listed, with the reason shown in the customer's language, so nobody wonders
where the free plan went. **Warn before a trial ends** sends one extra reminder a set number of hours
before the trial ends, and **Time left on a trial** decides whether the remaining trial time is
carried into the first paid period.

## Add-ons and mid-period changes

An add-on adds traffic, device slots, or squads. Create one in `/admin/catalog` → **Add-ons** →
**New add-on**, then tick it in each plan version that should offer it. A customer can buy it with
the plan at checkout, or later against a live subscription: in the bot under the subscription's
**Add-ons**, on the web under **Add to this subscription**. A mid-period purchase is priced by the
add-on's rule — full price, the share of the period left, or a daily rate. A traffic pack can be
given a lifetime in days.

During a period a customer can also:

| Change | Where | Money |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switch the traffic package up or down | **Traffic package** | Charges the new package for the time left, credits the unused part of the old one |
| Give back bought device slots | **Device limit** (web), **Fewer devices** (bot) | Credits the unused part to the wallet; refused while too many devices are connected |
| Remove a server add-on | **Server add-ons** | Credits the unused part to the wallet |
| Reset traffic for a fee | **Reset traffic** | Charged as a percentage of the plan price, set in **Paid traffic reset (% of the price)** on the version |

None of these moves the period's end. All of them are paid from the wallet first and through a
provider for the rest, and all reach Remnawave through the same queued, retried fulfillment as a
purchase.

## Plan changes and crediting the unused remainder

Upgrades and downgrades follow each version's policy: an upgrade either **replaces** the current
period (starting a fresh one now) or **extends** it (adding the new period on top); a downgrade
happens **immediately** or **at expiry**; either can be **forbidden**. **Allow upgrades** and **Allow
downgrades** turn them off for the whole installation. **Reset traffic on renewal** and **Reset
traffic on upgrade** are on by default.

By default an upgrade charges the full price of the new plan and a `replace` upgrade discards the time
left. Turn on **Credit the unused remainder on a plan change** to hand that time back as money: a
`replace` upgrade or an `immediate` downgrade credits what is left of the current period, prorated by
time at what the customer actually paid. The checkout shows the credit as **Unused time on your
current plan**, and any amount above the new price goes to the wallet. Time from a gift, a trial, a
code, or an operator's grant paid nothing and credits nothing. The switch is off by default.

## Grace periods and limited access

**Grace period (days)** on a version keeps access alive after the paid end, and Omniflow pushes the
end plus the grace to Remnawave, so the promise is real. Zero, the default, means expiry is
immediate. During grace the customer sees how long they have left to renew.

**Limited access** keeps a lapsed customer reachable instead of cutting them off entirely. Under
**Plan changes and resets** turn on **Limited access after expiry**, **Limited access when traffic
runs out**, or both, and set the **Limited access squad** (typically one that reaches Telegram
only), **Limited access traffic (GB)**, and **Limited access days**. The period is free, opens at
most once per subscription per trigger, and ends by itself. Any payment on the subscription restores
the full plan at once.

## Temporary squad access for N hours

To let a customer try a country, or to work around an outage, open a squad on one subscription for a
few hours. On the customer's card in `/admin/customers`, each subscription's actions panel has
**Temporary server access**: pick the squad, enter 1–720 hours, type a reason, and **Grant**. Active
grants are listed with **Revoke**. A `squad_access` promo code grants the same thing to whoever
redeems it. A paused or unprovisioned subscription cannot take a grant.

## Pausing

An operator can pause a subscription on request — "I am away for six weeks". On the subscription's
actions panel in the customer card, **Pause** disables the Remnawave user and stops the clock;
**Resume** moves the end forward by exactly the time paused and switches access back on. Both need a
reason, which is audited. The customer's screens say the subscription is paused and the remaining
days are kept.

There is no customer-facing pause except on daily plans, deliberately: a pause the holder controls
would turn a dated subscription into an indefinite one. Automatic renewal skips a paused
subscription, add-ons cannot be bought for one, and buying a renewal or a plan change while paused
builds on the days the pause preserved.

## Several subscriptions per customer

By default each customer holds one subscription. To let a customer hold several — a household with
separate device pools, two regions — set the environment and restart the API, bot, and worker:

```env
APP_MULTI_SUBSCRIPTION_ENABLED=true
APP_MAX_SUBSCRIPTIONS_PER_CUSTOMER=3
```

<Warning>
  The **Subscriptions** card in `/admin/settings` does not change the limit that is enforced; it only
  decides whether the web dashboard shows a subscription switcher. The limit comes from the two
  variables above.
</Warning>

A plan can narrow the limit further with its own per-customer cap, set through the API. With several
subscriptions the customer gets a switcher, every purchase and add-on names its target, each
subscription can be renamed, and reminders name the subscription they are about. Wallet, trial,
promotions, and referrals stay per customer. A customer can close any subscription except the first
from its web page; the time left is given up and nothing is refunded. Turning the switch off later
closes nothing.

## Automatic renewal

Auto-renewal is off until the customer turns it on for a subscription — in the bot's **Auto-renew** screen,
or on the web at `/account/renewal`. They choose the funding source (the wallet, or a
saved card where the provider supports it), how many days before expiry to charge (1, 3, or 7), and,
when the plan sells several renewal periods, which one. A failed charge is retried on a fixed
schedule and the customer is told when it matters. Operators cannot turn auto-renewal on for a
customer. [Payments and wallet](/features/payments-and-wallet) covers which providers can charge a
saved card.

## Reminders

The bot sends, per subscription, without any setup:

- expiry reminders 7, 3, 1, and 0 days before the end;
- traffic warnings at 80% and 100% of a limited allowance;
- a grace notice while the grace window runs;
- a recovery message with a one-tap button to buy the same plan again, for `APP_RECOVERY_WINDOW_HOURS`
  after grace ends (default 14 days);
- a notice when access is set up or setting it up failed.

These respect each customer's notification preferences and quiet hours, and are not counted against
marketing limits. The recovery message is Telegram-only.
