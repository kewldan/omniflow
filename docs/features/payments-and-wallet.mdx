Omniflow takes money through payment providers you connect, keeps a wallet balance for each
customer, and turns every paid order into VPN access. This page explains what each part does for
your business, how a customer sees it, and where you switch it on. The exact rules — state machines,
ledger entries, refusal codes — are on the reference pages linked at the end of each section.

<Frame caption="Finance: fulfilled orders with customer, provider, and amount, filters, and CSV export.">
  <img src="/screenshots/admin-finance.png" alt="Finance: fulfilled orders with customer, provider, and amount, filters, and CSV export." />
</Frame>

One rule shapes everything below: **money is recorded first, and access follows**. A payment that
settles is written to the books in one transaction together with the access it bought and a queued
job to set that access up in Remnawave. If Remnawave is down at that moment, the customer's money is
safe and access arrives when it comes back.

## How a customer pays

Every purchase — a plan, a renewal, an add-on, a top-up, a gift — is an order. At checkout the
customer's available wallet balance is applied first and a payment provider covers the rest. An
order the wallet covers in full is paid on the spot, with no provider at all. Both the bot and the
web checkout have an **apply wallet** toggle for a customer who would rather keep their balance.

A customer is shown a payment method only when you configured that provider **and** the plan has a
price in a currency the provider settles. The same plan can therefore cost a different amount
through different providers, and the checkout shows the price beside each method.

## Payment providers

Four rails are built in:

| Provider | Currencies | Turn it on with | Refunds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Telegram Stars | `XTR` | `APP_TELEGRAM_TOKEN`, the bot's own token | Full charge only |
| CryptoBot | Several fiat codes | `APP_CRYPTOBOT_TOKEN` | None |
| YooKassa | `RUB`, `USD`, `EUR` | `APP_YOOKASSA_SHOP_ID` and `APP_YOOKASSA_SECRET` | Full and partial |
| Manual | Any | Always registered and offered until switched off on **Payment providers** | Recorded only; you move the money |

Twenty-five more providers carried over from Bedolaga — among them Platega, FreeKassa, Kassa AI,
CloudPayments, Lava, Heleket, Cashera, Antilopay, WATA, and HOT Pay — are configured the same way:
each reads its own `APP_<SLUG>_*` variables. Setting none of a provider's variables leaves it off;
setting only some is a start-up error, so a half-configured provider is caught before the first
checkout. Restart the API and the worker after changing them. Providers that need a callback address
use `https://<APP_PUBLIC_URL>/v1/payments/webhooks/<slug>`.

Once a provider is configured, **Settings → Commerce** in the panel controls how it is shown:

- each provider's **Enabled** switch and **Display order**;
- **Payment buttons** — a name and description per button in both languages, minimum and maximum
  amounts, quick top-up amounts (the cabinet shows them only to a customer with no subscription;
  one with a subscription is offered its renewal price and, when the balance is short of it, the
  difference), and who sees it (by sign-in method, by first top-up, or by loyalty
  tier). Some providers can show SBP, card, and other methods as separate buttons through their
  `APP_<SLUG>_PAYMENT_METHODS` variable. In the cabinet each button carries the mark customers know
  it by: the SBP mark on any provider's SBP method, ЮKassa's on ЮKassa, CryptoBot's with the coins it
  takes, coins on other crypto methods, a star on Telegram Stars, and a plain card otherwise.

What the provider shows as the payment purpose can be worded with `APP_PAYMENT_SERVICE_NAME` and
the `APP_PAYMENT_ORDER_TEMPLATE`, `APP_PAYMENT_TOPUP_TEMPLATE`, and `APP_PAYMENT_RENEWAL_TEMPLATE`
templates.

**Manual payments** cover bank transfers, cash, and anything not integrated. Under **Settings →
Commerce → Manual payments** write the transfer instructions in Russian and English; the customer
sees them beside the manual method. A manual order stays payable for 72 hours and is settled when an
operator approves it through the operator-token API, with a reason that is audited. **Offer top-up
through support** on the same card adds a "top up through support" path to the wallet for customers
no provider serves.

<Note>
  A provider's notification alone never settles an order. Omniflow settles only on a verified
  notification or on its own authenticated status check, and for providers that support status
  checks the worker also asks about unsettled payments in the background, so a lost webhook does
  not leave a paid customer without access.
</Note>

## Telegram Stars

Stars need no merchant account: set the bot token, price a plan version in `XTR`, and the bot's order
screen offers an invoice. An installation that sells only for Stars sets `APP_DEFAULT_CURRENCY=XTR`.

To let Stars pay **ruble** prices too, turn on **Payouts → Exchange rates → Stars can pay ruble
prices** and choose the price of a Star: a fixed number of rubles, or derived from the USD/RUB rate
at Telegram's own price per Star. When a customer picks Stars for a ruble order, the Star amount is
fixed once and used for the invoice and the settlement, so a rate change in between moves nothing.
When no fresh rate is available and no manual value is set, Stars are simply not offered for ruble
orders.

<Warning>
  Stars have two limits to plan around. Telegram offers no status check, so a Stars payment settles
  only through the bot's update stream. And a Stars refund needs the Telegram account that paid,
  which a payment started from the web panel does not record — sell Stars purchases you may need to
  refund through the bot.
</Warning>

## The wallet and top-ups

Each customer has a balance per currency. It is never a stored number: it is the sum of recorded
movements, so every change has a line in the customer's history with its reason. Customers see three
figures — total, reserved by their own unpaid orders, and available to spend.

Customers top up from **Wallet → Top up** in the bot or from `/account/wallet` on the web: pick a
preset or type an amount, choose a provider, and pay. A top-up is credited exactly once, for exactly
what the provider settled — an over- or underpayment is credited as received and flagged to you.

Top-up limits are environment variables, read at start-up:

| Variable | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_ENABLED` | `true` | Offer top-ups at all |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_PRESETS` | `10000,30000,50000,100000` | One-tap amounts, in minor units |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_MINIMUM_MINOR` | `10000` | Smallest top-up |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_MAXIMUM_MINOR` | `5000000` | Largest top-up; `0` removes the ceiling |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_WINDOW_LIMIT_MINOR` | `10000000` | Most that may be credited in the window; `0` disables it |
| `APP_WALLET_TOPUP_WINDOW_HOURS` | `24` | Length of the rolling window |

<Warning>
  The **Wallet top-up** card in `/admin/settings` saves the same fields, but the limits actually
  enforced come from the variables above. Change the environment and restart.
</Warning>

A customer who is short of money can **save the purchase** instead of paying the difference through
a provider. The saved cart buys itself, at the current price, once a top-up covers it; a price that
has risen is not charged. Carts last `APP_CART_TTL_HOURS` (30 days by default).

From a customer's profile in `/admin/customers`, an operator with `finance.write` can **Adjust
balance** — a credit or debit with a reason the customer will see — or **Record as a payment** to
book money received outside a provider as a proper top-up. A balance never goes negative.

## Paying with the balance

Wallet money is spent automatically at checkout, before any provider. It also funds:

- **daily plans**, which charge the wallet one day at a time (see
  [Plans and subscriptions](/features/plans-and-subscriptions));
- **mid-period changes** — add-ons, traffic package changes, paid traffic resets;
- **automatic renewals** whose funding source is the wallet.

Credit coming back to a customer — the unused part of a plan or package on a change, a gift claimed
as credit, a referral reward — lands in the same wallet.

## Automatic renewal

Auto-renewal is per subscription and always the customer's choice: they turn it on in the bot's
**Auto-renew** screen or on the web at `/account/renewal`, and that action is the recorded
consent. Operators cannot turn it on for anyone.

The charge comes from the **wallet** by default. A customer can instead save a card and renew from
it, where both conditions hold: the provider can store a payment method — today **YooKassa** and
**HOT Pay** — and you have enabled recurring for your merchant account after a passing capability
test (`POST /v1/panel/settings/providers/{provider}/recurring`). The customer then sees **Save this
card for automatic renewal** on the web payment screen, or a "save the card" variant of the method
button in the bot.

The renewal is attempted three days before expiry by default (the customer can pick one, three, or
seven). A failed charge is retried after 4, 12, and 24 hours, configurable with
`APP_RENEWAL_RETRY_HOURS`; the customer is told from the second failure on, and after the last one
auto-renewal is suspended and they renew by hand. A wallet-funded renewal the balance will not cover
triggers a low-balance warning `APP_RENEWAL_LOW_BALANCE_WARNING_HOURS` (default 48) before the
charge. Failed charges are listed for review under **Finance → Failed charges**; nothing there
charges anyone again.

After a Bedolaga migration, **Finance → Previous bot** lists the YooKassa payments customers opened in
Bedolaga and paid after the cutover: credited to the customer's wallet automatically, or queued for
you when they could not be attributed, until you resolve them with a note. See
[Payments in flight at the cutover](/operations/migrate-from-bedolaga#payments-in-flight-at-the-cutover).

<Note>
  Providers that run their own subscription billing — Platega, Lava, Cashera, CisPay — are used for
  one-off payments only, because Omniflow cannot decide when they charge.
</Note>

## Refunds

A refund never rewrites history: it is a new record, and it takes back what the money bought. A
full refund of a subscription order removes the period it paid for; a refund of a top-up removes the
credit from the wallet; a partial refund changes only the money, leaving access to your judgement.
Refunds and chargebacks that providers report on their own — made in their dashboard, or disputed by
the bank — are recorded the same way, and a chargeback also retires the card saved from that payment.

<Warning>
  The **Issue a refund** form on `/admin/finance/{orderId}` records your decision and its reason in the
  audit log; it does not move money. Issue the refund itself with the operator-token API,
  `POST /v1/admin/payments/{paymentID}/refunds`, with an idempotency key and a reason.
</Warning>

What each provider allows: YooKassa and CloudPayments refund in full or in part, Telegram Stars only
in full, CryptoBot not at all. For the other providers, refund in the provider's own cabinet and
record it in Omniflow. The part of an order paid from the wallet is never refunded to a provider,
because it was never there.

## Self-employed receipts («Мой налог»)

If you are registered in Russia as self-employed (НПД), Omniflow can register each ruble payment in
«Мой налог» and give the payer a receipt, as Bedolaga's NaloGO integration does.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the settings">
    Go to **Payouts → Self-employed receipts** (needs `finance.write`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in your details">
    Enter your 12-digit taxpayer number and the «Мой налог» password, which is stored encrypted with
    `APP_DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`. Optionally change the service name (`Услуги VPN` by default) and limit
    receipts to some providers — leave out one that already issues a fiscal receipt itself, such as
    YooKassa with 54-FZ receipts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check and switch on">
    Press **Check sign-in**, then turn on **Issue receipts**. Only payments settled after this moment
    get a receipt.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Receipts are sent by the worker, never twice for one order, retried for twelve hours, and cancelled
when an order is fully refunded. They are listed on the **Payouts** page, announced in their own
operator topic, and the customer sees **Open the receipt** on the order page. Only the part paid
through a provider is receipted; the wallet part was receipted when it was topped up.

For YooKassa's own 54-FZ receipts, use the `APP_YOOKASSA_RECEIPTS` and `APP_YOOKASSA_RECEIPT_EMAIL`
variables instead.

## Guest purchases

A visitor without an account can buy a subscription at `/guest` with only an e-mail address — for
themselves or as a gift. Link to `/guest` from your own site. It is available when an e-mail transport
is configured and `APP_PUBLIC_URL` is set, with every provider that settles your shop currency except
Telegram Stars and manual transfers.

After payment the visitor receives an activation code by e-mail. They redeem it after signing in —
signing in by e-mail with the same address needs no password — and whoever redeems the code first
gets the subscription. All guest orders belong to one system account, the guest checkout, which you
will see in the customer list.

## When money and access fail independently

Payment, wallet, and Remnawave are separate systems, and Omniflow never lets a failure in one undo
another:

- **Remnawave is unreachable.** The payment and the access it bought are already recorded; the
  setup job retries until the panel answers. The customer sees "being set up", not an error, and
  nothing is refunded.
- **The payment does not match the order** — an underpayment, an overpayment, a duplicate, or a
  wallet balance that was spent elsewhere in the meantime. The money is recorded, the order does not
  settle, and you get an incident notice. Resolve it with a wallet adjustment or a refund.
- **A payment arrives after the order expired or was cancelled.** A matching amount is still
  settled; a payment on a cancelled order is also flagged to you.
- **An add-on is paid but the subscription has ended.** The money stays recorded and you get a
  fulfillment-failure notice; nothing rolls back.

Failed setup jobs are visible under **/admin → System → Jobs** with **Retry** and **Cancel**.
