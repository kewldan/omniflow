A referral programme pays customers for bringing somebody who then buys. In Omniflow it covers the
whole ladder an owner usually needs: a one-off reward for an invite, a share of every payment the
friend makes afterwards, a cash-out of what was earned, a second level, contests, and a partner track
for bloggers and channel owners who promote the service for a personal rate.

Every payout is a ledger posting in the customer's wallet, so it shows in their balance history, can
be withdrawn, and can be taken back if the referral turns out to be abuse. This page explains what
each part does and where to switch it on. The exact rules, routes, and refusals are on
[Referrals and loyalty](/commerce/referrals-loyalty).

## The referral programme

Each customer has a personal referral code. The bot gives them a link of the form
`t.me/<bot>?start=ref_<CODE>`, and the web panel gives a link to its sign-in page with `?ref=<CODE>`.
A friend who opens either link is attributed to the inviter. The pair is fixed at the first
attribution: a later link does not move the friend to somebody else, and nobody can invite
themselves.

The referral counts — **qualifies** — when the friend's first subscription order is paid with money
from outside the wallet: a purchase, an extension, a renewal, or a plan change. A wallet top-up, a
shop purchase, or a gift does not qualify it. Qualification and the reward are written in the same
transaction that settles the payment, so a retried webhook never pays twice.

### What an invite earns

You choose what each side gets, independently:

| Reward | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Balance | An amount credited to the wallet, in the programme currency |
| Days | That many days added to the customer's subscription, on the plan of their latest subscription |
| Both, with a choice | With **Let customers choose days or balance when both are set** on, the customer picks one on their referral screen; without a choice, days win |

A day reward needs something to extend: an inviter who never had a subscription receives nothing,
and the friend's payment is not held up by that. Day rewards move no money, so they cannot be
withdrawn and are not taken back when a referral is rejected in review.

### Commission on every payment

Beside or instead of the one-off rewards, the inviter can earn a share of what their friend pays,
for as long as they keep paying. The **Commission on payments** block sets the rate (a separate rate
for the first payment is optional), how many of one friend's payments earn, the smallest payment
that earns, whether subscriptions, wallet top-ups, or both count, and optional tiers that raise the
rate once an inviter has enough qualified referrals.

Commission is computed on what the payment provider settled — the wallet part of an order never
earns — and rounded down. A programme that pays only commission is a valid programme.

### Setting it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the referral card">
    Go to **Marketing → Referrals and loyalty** (`/admin/marketing?section=referrals`); the
    referral programme is the first card. Changing it
    needs `settings.write`, because it decides what the business pays.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the rewards">
    Set the currency, the inviter and invitee rewards in balance or days, and the commission if you
    want one. Optionally cap rewarded invites per inviter, limit how long an attribution can still
    qualify (90 days by default), set an expiry on credited rewards, and link your programme terms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable and save">
    A programme that is enabled but pays nothing on either side is refused: the panel shows a "pays
    nothing" alert and keeps **Save** disabled. Beneath the form you see what the programme has
    produced so far — attributed, qualified, rejected, and the money paid.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Turning the programme on later does not pay for orders that already settled. Invites keep being
  recorded from the bot while it is off, but a web invite is recorded only while the programme is on
  and only for a customer who has not paid for anything yet.
</Note>

### What the customer sees

In the bot, **🎁 Invite** opens the invitation screen with the customer's code and link and a
**📷 QR code** button that sends the link as a QR picture. The picture is drawn by the bot itself,
so the link never passes through an outside QR service. The same screen has **🤝 Partner programme**
and, once a game is switched on, **🎲 Daily games**.

On the web panel, **Invites** has two places with a switch at the top of both. **Invite a friend**
(`/account/referrals`) opens on one card: what the friend and the customer get in one sentence, the
code, the link, and its QR code. Below it are what invites and commissions earned over 7 days,
30 days, and all time; **How rewards are paid**; the withdrawal form; and the friends invited — when
each joined, whether they paid, and what they earned the customer. **Partner programme**
(`/account/referrals/partner`) holds the application and the partner's rate; its switch is shown
only when the installation runs the programme. Friends are never identified to the inviter. The
customer is told in the bot when a friend joins and when a reward is credited; these notices cannot
be switched off.

**How rewards are paid** is built from your settings, never written by hand: a day reward is added to
the subscription; a money reward is credited to the wallet, where it pays for a subscription; and
whether it can also be withdrawn, from what minimum, how often, and when unspent money expires
follow from **Payouts** and the programme's expiry. With withdrawals off, the screen says the reward
cannot be withdrawn. The reward choice, when offered, sits in the same section. On the partner page
the rate is said to be credited to the balance; withdrawal is mentioned there only when the whole
balance may be withdrawn.

**Settings → Marketing** can also add **🎟 I have an invite code** to a newcomer's welcome in the
bot, so somebody who was told a code rather than sent a link can still be attributed.

## Review and reversal

Some referrals are abuse: a customer inviting their own second account, or a ring of accounts paying
each other. A pair can be marked **held** for a look, cleared, or **rejected**. Rejecting writes a
correcting ledger entry for every money reward on the pair — commissions included — and requires a
note, because it takes money back from a customer and the note is what you quote to them.

<Warning>
  A reversal can take a wallet negative. That is deliberate: otherwise an abusive reward would be
  safe once it was spent. Day rewards are not taken back.
</Warning>

Review is done through the `/v1/panel/referrals/review` API with the `risk.read` and `risk.write`
permissions; there is no panel screen for it yet. Signals — notes that hold a pair for review — can
only ask for a person and never reject anything on their own, and nothing raises them automatically
today.

The marketing screen does have two related tools: a leaderboard of top inviters, and **lost
referrals** — friends who paid but never qualified, with the likely cause — each with a **Credit**
button that grants what the programme pays. On a customer's card, **Referral administration** lets
you attach a customer to an inviter by code, detach a pair that has paid nothing out, and set a
personal commission rate.

## Withdrawals

A customer can ask to be paid out what their invitations earned. It is off until you switch it on
in **Payouts** (`/admin/payouts`), where you set the currency, the minimum amount, the number of days
between requests, and whether only referral earnings may leave the wallet (the default) or the whole
balance.

The customer asks on the web panel's referral screen with an amount, a method — card, SBP, crypto,
or other — and the details to pay to. A request is refused below the minimum, above what may be
withdrawn, inside the waiting period, or while another request is open.

- **The amount is held at once.** The request and a wallet debit are written together, so money
  promised for a payout cannot also be spent on a subscription. Rejecting the request, or the
  customer cancelling it, puts the money back.
- **The payout happens outside Omniflow.** Approving and marking paid move no money in the system;
  you pay by whatever means you use and record that you did.
- **A person decides.** Every request carries a 0–100 risk score with its reasons — referral income
  with no paying referral, nearly all income from one friend, a very new account, and others. The
  score is advice; nothing is approved or rejected automatically.
- **Details are sealed.** The queue shows only the method; **Show details** unseals them for an
  operator with `finance.write` and writes that to the audit trail.

Deciding needs `finance.write`. A rejection needs a note to the customer, and a customer with a
verified e-mail address is mailed each decision.

## Second level and referral contests

Both live in **Marketing → Second level and referral contests**.

**Second level.** When an inviter is credited a money reward or a commission, the person who invited
*them* receives a share of it, from 1 to 50 %. It is paid within a minute by the worker, once per
first-level reward, and reversed when that reward is reversed. Switching it on pays from the day
before onwards, never the whole history. Day rewards have no second level, and there is no third.

**Contests.** A contest has a title, a time window, and a prize per place, up to ten places. Each
inviter is ranked by referrals that qualified inside the window and were not rejected; a tie goes to
whoever got there first. Customers see running contests on their referral screen — the top ten and
their own place, never who the others are. When the window closes, an operator with `finance.write`
presses **Finish and pay**, which credits the prizes to wallets once.

## Partners

A partner is a customer who promotes the service — a channel, a blog, a community — and is paid a
personal commission rate instead of the programme's.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The customer applies">
    In the bot, **🤝 Partner programme** on the invitation screen applies in one tap. On the web,
    **Partner programme** (`/account/referrals/partner`) takes where they will promote the service and a
    message for you. One application may wait at a time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You decide">
    **Marketing → Partner applications** lists waiting applications first, with each applicant's
    referrals so far. **Approve** takes a rate and an optional first-payment rate (0–100 %) and sets
    them as the customer's personal rate; **Decline** records the decision and changes nothing. Both
    need `marketing.write`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The partner earns">
    Commissions on their friends' payments are paid exactly like any other commission, so
    withdrawals, review, and reversal all apply. Removing the personal rate on the customer's card
    ends the partnership, and they may apply again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A personal rate can also be set directly on any customer's card, without an application.

## The referral network

On a customer's card, the **Referrals** tab shows **Referral network**: who invited them and that
person's inviter, and two levels below — the friends they invited and the friends those invited.
Each level shows how many were invited, how many qualified, and what they earned the customer; each
member shows when they joined, whether they qualified or were rejected, and what their payments
earned. It is the place to answer "why did this customer earn so much" before approving a
withdrawal.

## Campaign links and attribution

A referral link is one of several `/start` links the bot understands, and they do different jobs:

| Link | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `?start=ref_<CODE>` | Attributes the newcomer to the inviter who owns the code |
| `?start=c_<code>` | Credits an advertising campaign with the sign-up and can grant a sign-up bonus |
| `?start=utm_<source>__<medium>__<campaign>` | Records UTM tags, which then follow the customer's orders into the channel report |
| `?start=coupon_<CODE>` | Redeems a promo code on opening the bot |

A customer has one inviter and one advertising campaign at most. When registration is by invitation
(the `invite` mode under **Registration** in the installation settings), a referral link is one of
the ways a newcomer may get in, unless referrals are excluded there. Advertising campaigns are covered on [Promotions and offers](/features/promotions-and-offers).
