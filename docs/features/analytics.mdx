title: Analytics and reporting
description: See what sold, whether the money arrived, which advertising paid off, which node is filling up, and what Omniflow itself reports to the project
---

Omniflow gives you several views of the business, each answering one question and each built
so that two people reading it reach the same conclusion. The dashboard shows the state of things
right now. Sales reporting shows what was sold over any period and whether payments are
healthy. Advertising measurement connects an advertisement to the order it produced. Traffic
reporting shows which node is filling up and who uses the most. Anomaly detection points at
customers worth a second look. Scheduled summaries bring the main figures to your Telegram chat.

Separately, Omniflow sends one anonymous heartbeat a day to the project. That is covered at the
end of this page, with how to switch it off.

## Rules that apply to every figure

- **Provider money and wallet credit are never added together.** Money taken through a payment
  provider, wallet balance spent, and refunds are separate figures everywhere. A wallet balance
  was already counted when it was topped up, so a single "revenue" number would count it twice.
- **A sale belongs to the moment it was paid**, and that moment never moves. Recording a refund
  in March does not move a February sale into March.
- **Refunds are reported on the day they were issued**, not against the sale they reverse.
- **Money is reported per currency.** Nothing converts one currency into another to make a total.

## The dashboard

`/admin` is the operations overview: customers, entitlements, payments in flight, support and
fulfillment queues, and a revenue chart per currency. The period switch above it covers a day, a
week, 30 days (the default), 90 days, or a year, and every windowed tile is compared with the
period of the same length before it. Every tile states what it counts — "active
customers" means customer records that are neither suspended nor deleted, not people with a live
subscription — and every tile with a list behind it opens that list.

An operator with `finance.read` also sees **Customer balances now**: what customers hold on their
wallets at this moment, per currency, with how many customers hold a positive balance. It is a
balance, not a figure over the window, and it is not revenue: that money was counted when it was
topped up and is still owed as service.

Tiles that count over the window — new customers, succeeded and failed payments, provider money —
also show the change against the previous thirty days, a small line of the same thirty days by day,
and a short note when the shape is worth mentioning ("Last 7 days: 40% above the usual pace").
Green and red mean good and bad for that measure, so more failed payments is red. Renewals due
shows the next thirty days as bars instead. See
[the dashboard reference](/operations/admin-operations#dashboard) for how each is counted.

The **needs action** list is built from the same figures as the tiles and links to the screen
that resolves each item. Times are UTC.

## Sales and payment health

`/admin/reports` is where the period moves: 7, 30, 90, or 365 days, or any range up to two years.
Days are counted in your browser's timezone, so "sales on the third" means your third. Reading it
needs `finance.read`.

| Tab | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Sales** | Trial conversion; returning payers; sales by kind (new, renewal, extension, upgrade, add-on, top-up, gift, digital goods); sales by plan and billing period; provider money by day; refunds issued |
| **Payment health** | Whether the money arrived: settlement rate, completion rate, payments still in flight, time to settle, and webhook intake per provider |
| **Channels** | Where paying customers came from — see below |

Payment health separates two rates on purpose. **Settlement** is the provider's own success rate
on payments it was asked to take. **Completion** also counts customers who abandoned checkout. A
falling settlement rate is a provider problem; a falling completion rate may be just a campaign
reaching people who were never going to buy. A rate with no decided payments is shown as absent,
not as 0%, and every rate carries its sample size.

Trial conversion is a cohort figure: of trials claimed in the period, how many of those customers
have paid since. A period ending today therefore reads low, because yesterday's trials have had
one day to convert.

Returning payers is the retention figure: of the customers who paid in the period, how many had
paid before it. After a [Bedolaga migration](/operations/migrate-from-bedolaga) the payments the
old bot took count as earlier payments, so a customer coming back in the first month is not
mistaken for a new one; what the old bot took inside the period is shown on the same card and is
never added to the sales. See [Sales reporting](/operations/sales-reporting).

**Export CSV** downloads the whole sales report as one file with a `section` column. Amounts are
in minor units, exactly as stored — divide by your currency's own factor.

## Advertising measurement

A counter on your website sees the visit but never the sale, because payment happens on the
backend, often days later. Omniflow closes that gap by carrying the advertising click identifier
(`gclid`, `yclid`, `fbclid`, and others) and `utm_*` tags from the landing visit to the order the
visitor eventually pays for.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your counters">
    In `/admin/settings/analytics`, enter a Yandex Metrica counter number or a Google Analytics 4
    measurement ID. You supply an identifier, never a script, so nobody with panel access can run
    arbitrary code in customers' browsers. Site-verification tags for Google, Yandex, Bing,
    Facebook, and Antilopay's Apple Pay check go on the same screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn measurement on">
    Counters and click capture run only for visitors who agree to them, through a banner with equal
    accept and decline buttons. With no counter configured, no banner is shown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the Channels tab">
    `/admin/reports` → **Channels** lists settled orders per channel with revenue and refunds. The
    **Uploadable** column counts orders that carry the platform's own click identifier; if it is
    much lower than **Orders**, a redirect is probably stripping the query string from your links.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload conversions yourself">
    **Export for upload** downloads a CSV in the shape offline-conversion imports expect, refunds
    included. Omniflow never sends anything to an advertising network on its own.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Campaigns inside Telegram

A post in a channel or a broadcast from another bot has no web visit to carry a tag. For those,
**Marketing → Advertising campaigns (/start)** creates a code and a link
`t.me/<bot>?start=c_<code>`. A new customer arriving through it is credited to the campaign once,
and can receive a sign-up bonus: wallet credit, VPN days, the trial, or days of a chosen plan.
Each campaign shows sign-ups, trials, paying customers, conversion, and revenue net of refunds,
and **Report** opens daily charts for the last 7 to 365 days.

UTM tags and click identifiers also travel through `/start`: `?start=utm_<source>__<medium>__<campaign>`,
`?start=yclid_<id>`, or `?start=gclid_<id>`. Orders from those customers count in the Channels tab
and the export like web orders.

## Traffic

`/admin/traffic` reads Remnawave live on every request; nothing is stored in Omniflow, so there is
no history.

- **Nodes**, sorted by how close each is to its traffic limit. A node with no limit shows no
  saturation figure rather than 0%.
- **Heaviest users**: the top fifty in the current cycle, with lifetime use beside it, each linked
  to the customer who holds the subscription. When the scan stops before the end, the screen says
  how many users it covered.
- **Devices**: platforms and client applications, from Remnawave's own census.
- **Subscription requests**: when, from which address, and by which application one user's
  subscription was fetched — the way to spot a link being used where it should not be.

Both lists export as CSV with byte counts unscaled. Reading the screen needs `customers.read`.

Optional worker passes can watch for sudden growth — users whose traffic grew by more than a
threshold in a short interval or in a day — and for users with more devices than their limit,
and post them to the **Usage monitoring** topic. They need `APP_VALKEY_URL` and are off until you
set a threshold; the `APP_TRAFFIC_MONITOR_*` settings are on
[Traffic reporting](/operations/traffic-reporting).

## Anomaly detection

Under **Risk**, four metrics can be watched per customer: paid order value, refunded value,
qualified referrals, and observed traffic. Each rule has a window, a warning threshold, an alert
threshold, and a minimum sample, so a new installation does not alert on its first three orders.
The worker evaluates rules every fifteen minutes, and a condition that persists is one signal per
window, not one per run.

A signal is evidence for an operator, never an automatic sanction: nothing in this area suspends,
refunds, or blocks anybody. Signals go to the **Anomalies** topic without naming the customer; the
panel is where somebody with `risk.read` looks the subject up.

## Summaries in Telegram

The **Scheduled reports** section in `/admin/settings/operations` sends daily, weekly (Mondays),
and monthly (on the 1st) summaries to the **Reports** topic at a local time you choose: new
customers, trials, paid orders, top-ups, revenue per currency, tickets opened and waiting, and
active subscriptions. Nothing is sent until you choose the periods. Individual purchases, renewals,
top-ups, and refunds have their own topics; see [Operator notifications](/operations/operator-notifications).

## Telemetry

Once a day the API sends one HTTPS request to the project's collector. It carries an anonymous
installation identifier, the build version, the service name (`api`), the operating system, and
the CPU architecture — nothing else. No customer, amount, domain, IP address, traffic figure, or
row count is ever included. It exists so maintainers know which platforms and versions are in use.

Telemetry is **on by default**. To switch it off, set `APP_TELEMETRY_ENABLED=false` on the API and
restart it. Spell it `false`: an unrecognised value falls back to the default, which is on.

<Warning>
  The telemetry card in `/admin/settings/operations` can show "disabled" while heartbeats are being
  sent, because it reads a stored settings document rather than the running configuration. The
  API start-up log is the authoritative answer: it prints either `anonymous telemetry enabled` or
  `anonymous telemetry disabled`.
</Warning>

Telemetry is the project's, not yours, and it is unrelated to advertising measurement: your
counters and channel reports are never reported to the project.
