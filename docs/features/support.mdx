Support in Omniflow is one desk with two front doors. A customer writes from the Telegram bot or
from the web cabinet; an operator answers from `/admin/support` in the admin panel; the reply reaches
the customer in the chat and on the web. It is one conversation per ticket, whichever surface the
customer happened to open, so a reply read in Telegram is not still unread in the browser.

This page covers what the customer sees, how to set up and work the desk, how operators are told
about new work, and the information pages that answer questions before they become tickets. The full
reference is [Support desk](/operations/support-desk).

## How customers reach you

### From the bot

`/support` or `💬 Support` lists the customer's recent conversations with their status and unread
count, and offers `✍️ New request`, plus two shortcuts that say what the request is about: a payment
problem goes to the `payment` queue, and urgent raises the priority to `high`.

- A message is up to 4000 characters, with up to five photos or documents of at most 10 MB each.
  Video, voice notes, animations, and stickers are refused rather than silently dropped.
- The bot warns before the customer writes: never send passwords, tokens, or the subscription link.
- A customer who receives a reply and simply types an answer has it added to their most recent live
  request from the last 72 hours, with a confirmation naming the request and a button to open it.
- Replying to a resolved request reopens it; a closed one has to be reopened or replaced.

### From the web cabinet

`/account/support` shows the same conversations. **New conversation** opens a ticket with a subject,
a message, an optional topic and urgency, and attachments. The cabinet loads its limits from the API
before the form is usable, so the counters show exactly what will be accepted.

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Open conversations at once | 5 |
| Subject / message | 120 / 4000 characters |
| Attachment | 5 MiB; PNG, JPEG, WebP, PDF, or plain text |
| Web uploads per conversation | 20 |

Customers also get explicit **Close** and **Reopen** controls. Internal operator notes are never
returned to them — they live in a separate table that no customer-facing query reads.

### Choosing what the bot offers

The support section on `/admin/settings/integrations` decides the bot's support screen:

| Field | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `mode` | `both` (tickets and a contact link, the default), `tickets` only, or `contact` only |
| `introRu`, `introEn` | Your introduction above the ticket list, per language |

The contact link is `supportContact` in the branding section, falling back to `APP_SUPPORT_URL`.
`contact` mode turns the bot's support screen into a single link to your own account or chat.

## Working the desk

`/admin/support` has two tabs: **Queue**, the workspace, and **Report**, the response-time figures.
It needs `support.read` to open and `support.write` to act.

### Queues and their promises

A queue is a named bucket with a first-response target and a resolution target. Each queue card shows
the open count, how many nobody has taken, and how many are already past the target, and clicking a
card filters the list. A target of zero means the queue promises nothing and never breaches.

Every installation starts with a default `general` queue, and a `payment` queue customers can choose
when they report a payment problem.

<Note>
  Queues, tags, and canned responses have no editing form in the panel. They are defined through
  the API — `PUT /v1/panel/support/queues`, `PUT /v1/panel/support/tags`, and
  `PUT /v1/panel/support/canned` — and need `settings.write` rather than `support.write`, because
  they are configuration: the person answering tickets should not redefine what their queue promises.
</Note>

### A ticket, from arrival to close

<Steps>
  <Step title="Take it">
    **Take it** in the conversation header assigns the ticket to you; **Release** puts it down
    again. Priority (`low`, `normal`, `high`, `urgent`), queue, and tags are set from the same
    header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer, or leave a note">
    The conversation has two separate boxes: a reply with **Send**, and an amber internal note with
    **Add note**. They are deliberately two boxes rather than one with a toggle. **Insert canned
    reply** fills the reply box with a prepared answer. Sending a reply on an open ticket marks it
    **Answered**; the customer's next message opens it again. The number beside **Support** in the
    sidebar counts open tickets assigned to you or to nobody; it updates right after a reply and
    otherwise once a minute.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch it deliver">
    A reply appears as `queued` and turns `delivered` once the bot has pushed it into the customer's
    chat, which happens within about 30 seconds. A reply that cannot be pushed stays in the
    conversation and is labelled with the reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolve or close">
    Setting `resolved` or `closed` writes a short system message telling the customer, in their
    language, that they can reply or reopen. A customer who replies to a resolved ticket reopens it,
    and the reopen is counted.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Other tools on the same screen: **Merge** folds a duplicate ticket into another of the same
customer's, picked from a searchable list of their tickets; **Hide from customer** takes a wrong reply or a pasted secret out of the customer's view
(the desk keeps it, struck through); **Close all open tickets** closes everything after a typed
confirmation. The **No support** restriction on the customer card stops one customer from opening
tickets or writing into them.

<Warning>
  The panel inserts the English body of a canned response whatever the customer's language. When
  answering a Russian-speaking customer, paste the Russian text yourself.
</Warning>

### A live conversation

On the web, support is a live conversation. While a customer has the cabinet open, an operator's
reply appears in their conversation within seconds, and the operator sees the customer's messages
the same way in the admin panel. In Telegram, the reply arrives as a `🛟 Support reply` message with
a button to open the request.

<Warning>
  A customer with no Telegram account linked — one who signs in only by e-mail or an OIDC provider —
  is never pushed a reply. They read it when they open `/account/support`, and the cabinet tells them
  so. The desk marks such a message as undeliverable, so you know not to wait for a push.
</Warning>

## Attachments and how long they are kept

Attachments are stored two ways, and the difference matters for operators.

- **From the bot**, Omniflow records Telegram's file reference and safe metadata — name, type,
  size — and, with an object store configured (`APP_S3_*`), the bot copies the file into it within a
  minute. The desk then shows the photo in the conversation and offers a download, as for a web
  upload. Without an object store the file stays in Telegram and the desk labels it **in Telegram**.
- **From the web**, the file is stored by the installation — in the object store when one is
  configured, otherwise in `APP_SUPPORT_ATTACHMENT_DIR` (`/var/lib/omniflow/support-attachments` by
  default) — and has a **Download** button for anyone with `support.read`.

Attachments expire 90 days after upload, and the worker's hourly retention sweep removes them. This
is not configurable.

<Warning>
  The `api` and `worker` processes must mount the same attachment directory. The api writes the
  files and the worker's sweep removes them; if they disagree, rows are deleted while files stay and
  the disk grows without bound.
</Warning>

## Operator notifications

If you have connected an operator group, the bot posts support events into its **💬 Support
tickets** topic: every new ticket, every later customer message, and every ticket whose first
response is overdue. A new-ticket notice reads like the previous bot's: the ticket, the customer's
Telegram name, ID, and handle, the queue, the subject, and the customer's message, with buttons to
message the customer, open their profile and the ticket, close the ticket, and block the customer.
A reply notice quotes the new message. Setting up the group and who may press the buttons are
covered in [Operator notifications](/operations/operator-notifications).

The subject and message are the customer's own words in a chat that is kept for years. Switch
`notifyIncludeText` off and the notices carry everything else but those two.

Four switches in the support section narrow the stream, all on by default:

| Field | Turns off |
| --- | --- |
| `notifyNewTicket` | Notices for new tickets |
| `notifyCustomerReply` | Notices for later customer messages |
| `notifySlaBreach` | The once-per-ticket notice that the first response is overdue |
| `notifyIncludeText` | The ticket's subject and the customer's message in the notices |

The operator group section can also switch the whole support stream off or cap its volume. The
daily, weekly, and monthly reports in the same group include tickets opened and waiting.

## The response-time report

**Report** shows open, unassigned, and breached tickets, tickets resolved in the window, and the
median first response, for 7, 30, or 90 days, with each definition printed under its figure. Below
them, replies per operator are drawn as a bar chart with the full table — replies, open tickets,
resolved tickets, and each operator's median first response. Replies count every message an operator
wrote, including on tickets assigned to somebody else.

## Customer satisfaction

Ten minutes after a ticket is resolved, the bot asks its customer once for a mark from 1 to 5, as long
as the ticket was resolved in the last three days and the bot can reach them. Below the workload in
**Report**, over the same 7, 30, or 90 days, are **CSAT** (the share of fours and fives), the average,
how many marks arrived and the response rate, the distribution, and the same per operator the ticket
was assigned to. A resolved or closed conversation in the web cabinet offers the same 1–5 question
under the thread; whichever surface the customer answers first holds the mark, and the bot does not
ask again. See
[Subscription and acquisition reporting](/operations/business-analytics#support-satisfaction).

## AI-drafted replies

Not usable in this release. The AI features for support — ticket summaries, suggested replies,
rewriting, translation, and classification — can be configured on `/admin/settings/ai`, each off until
you enable it and choose a provider and a model. Their code exists, but no screen or endpoint in this
build calls them: the support desk has no control that requests a draft, and the only thing on the AI
settings screen that reaches a provider is **Test connection**. Enabling them changes nothing about
how tickets are answered, and the desk works fully without AI.

<Note>
  The design these features follow is documented so you know what to expect when they are wired in:
  everything they produce is a draft labelled as generated, nothing is sent without an operator
  accepting it, and ticket text passes through a redaction gateway first. See
  [AI assistant](/features/ai-assistant) and [AI governance](/operations/ai-governance).
</Note>

## Information pages and FAQ

The cheapest ticket is the one never written. `/admin/content` publishes documents at addresses on
your own installation — `/pages/faq`, `/pages/terms`, `/pages/offer`, `/pages/privacy`, and any slug
you need.

- A page is a draft, published, or published but unlisted. Withdrawing is reversible; deleting
  takes the address itself, so it needs the slug typed to confirm.
- The body uses a small, safe syntax — headings, bullets, numbered items, and `https` links — and is
  never rendered as HTML.
- A page in one language still answers for readers of the other.

Customers find these pages through **📄 Rules and FAQ** and `/faq` in the bot, which open each page
as text inside Telegram, and from the account screen in the web cabinet. The pages are public and
server-rendered, which is what a payment provider's reviewer needs. See
[Information pages](/operations/information-pages).

## Permissions

| Action | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| Read queues, tickets, canned replies, and the report | `support.read` |
| Assign, move, prioritise, reply, note, tag, merge, change status, hide a message | `support.write` |
| Define queues, tags, and canned replies; change the support section | `settings.write` |
| Block a customer from support | `customers.write` |

Reading and answering are separate on purpose: a finance operator investigating a refund can read the
conversation without being able to reply in the desk's voice.
