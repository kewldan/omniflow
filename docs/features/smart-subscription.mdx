title: Smart subscription
description: Servers grouped by country with a fastest-server group, a whitelist-bypass fallback, and routing each customer sets up for their own subscription
---

A Remnawave subscription hands a VPN app one entry per host, and that is all a customer can work
with: a flat list, the same for everybody. The smart subscription turns it into something a
customer can shape. Their app gets one group per country and a "Fastest" group that picks the
best server on its own. The whitelist-bypass servers have no group of their own: they stand behind
every group and take over when a mobile network only lets approved sites through. Each customer decides which countries they see at all, which ones the
automatic choice may use, and which domains go direct, through the tunnel, or nowhere.

It is **off until you turn it on**, and turning it off again is safe at any time.

## How it works

While the module is on, customers are handed an Omniflow link instead of the panel's:
`https://<your address>/v1/sub/<short UUID>`. The short UUID is the panel's own, so the link is
exactly as secret as the panel's, and re-issuing the link in the cabinet or the bot re-issues both.

When an app refreshes the subscription, Omniflow asks Remnawave for it on the app's behalf —
forwarding its User-Agent and device headers, so your response rules and the device limit apply as
before — and rewrites what goes back:

- **Xray JSON apps** (Happ, INCY, v2RayTun, Streisand, V2Box, v2rayN, FoXray, and similar) get one config
  per group. Each group is a balancer that times its servers and picks the least loaded; when none
  of them answers, it falls back to the whitelist-bypass servers, and then to every server. A
  server counts as not answering when the check through it takes longer than five seconds: a chain
  through a relay, or the bypass itself, needs two to three and a half from Russia.
  In a group of several servers each subscription keeps one of them, the same on every refresh and
  spread evenly across subscriptions, and every connection leaves through it while its check
  answers within two seconds. Sites see one address rather than a new country on each connection;
  when the kept server slows down or stops answering, the group's balancer takes over.
- **Every other app** gets the panel's own answer, with the servers the customer hid taken out when
  it is a plain list of links.
- An answer the module cannot rewrite — a limit or expiry notice, an encrypted or unknown format —
  reaches the app exactly as Remnawave sent it.

Hosts whose nodes are all disconnected are left out while anything else answers. Nothing about your
hosts is copied into Omniflow; the list is read from Remnawave once a minute.

<Note>
  Changes reach a device when its app refreshes the subscription. Most apps do that on a timer; a
  customer can also refresh by hand.
</Note>

## Turning it on

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the link address">
    Open **Smart subscription** in the sidebar. Links are built on `APP_PUBLIC_URL` followed by
    `/v1/sub/`, or on the link base you enter. If neither is set, the screen says so and customers
    keep the panel's link.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the hosts">
    The table lists your Remnawave hosts with the country read from each one's flag or name. Correct
    a country where it was read wrong. Give the hosts that work on whitelisted mobile networks the
    **Whitelist bypass** role, and hide test or retiring hosts with **Hidden**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check which node each host exits from">
    The **Services** column is what opens through each host: Gemini, YouTube, Reddit without
    signing in, ChatGPT, Claude, Netflix, Spotify, and TikTok. It comes from Remnawave's own
    geocheck, which Omniflow's worker runs on every connected node every 6 hours while the module
    is on; nothing has to be marked by hand. Google's placement matters most: an address it puts
    in Russia loses Gemini and gets YouTube's Russian region, whatever country the panel shows.

    A host is shown its own node's results. A host whose inbound forwards to another node (a chain
    through a second exit) leaves from that other node, so pick it under **Exit node**. A host
    that runs on several nodes needs one picked too. The exit node also decides the customer's
    ping estimate, below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up the service rules">
    On the **Rules** tab, decide which cards customers see on their Routing tab and how each one
    starts. See [Service rules](#service-rules) below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn it on and save">
    From now on the cabinet and the bot hand out the new link. Customers who already imported the
    panel's link keep it until they import the new one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  If you use subscription mirrors (`APP_SUBSCRIPTION_MIRRORS`), each mirror must forward `/v1/sub/`
  to Omniflow. A mirror that only proxies the Remnawave subscription page will not serve the new
  link.
</Warning>

## What customers see

A **Make the VPN yours** card on the cabinet's home screen and on a subscription's page, **Servers
and routing** in the desktop menu, and the same entry under **Profile → Connection** on a phone open
the customer's own screen; from the menu, a customer with several subscriptions picks one first. The first visit opens with a three-step **How it works** card (servers, routing, save
and refresh in the app) and the reassurance that the defaults already work; it stays closed once
closed and comes back from **How it works** next to the title. Unsaved changes float a **Save** bar
above the tab bar, and a save shows how to refresh the subscription in Happ, INCY, and other apps,
since the change reaches the device only then. The screen has:

- **Servers** lists their countries. **Shown** keeps a country in the subscription; **Auto** lets the
  Fastest group use it. A country can be shown but kept out of Auto — for example, so the automatic
  choice never lands in one country but it is still there to pick by hand. Expanding a country lists
  its servers. The ping is measured as soon as the screen opens (**Refresh** measures again) and
  estimates the round trip through the VPN to common sites. The browser times the way to each server's entry, which is about two round trips
  over TCP (one for a Hysteria server); Omniflow adds the rest of the route from the same geocheck:
  the hop from the entry node to the exit node, estimated from the distance between them (or a
  millisecond when they share a data centre), the exit node's median round trip to common sites,
  and a millisecond per node. It is within a few milliseconds where checked, but it is only as
  good as the timing: with the VPN on, the timing runs through it. The whitelist bypass shows the
  raw timing, since it is reached through a relay the panel does not list. Under each country are
  the services Remnawave's check found: green works, amber works with limits (YouTube treating the
  address as Russian) or on only some of the country's servers, struck out doesn't.
- **My server** pins one server: it becomes the first group in the app, falling back to the rest
  of its country and then the whitelist bypass if it stops answering. The screen tells customers
  why they might want it: one exit address means fewer captchas, fewer sign-outs and
  "confirm it's you" prompts, and banks and shops that stop treating them as suspicious. A server
  is pinned from the pin next to it in an expanded country.
- **Whitelist bypass** can be switched off by a customer who never needs it. **Whitelist mode**,
  if you offer it (**General → Whitelist mode for customers**, off by default), is for the days a
  mobile network lets only approved sites through: Fastest starts on the bypass servers and keeps
  the ordinary ones as its fallback. It is off by default because a customer who forgets to turn
  it off keeps spending the bypass servers' traffic.
- **Routing** shows the service rules as cards grouped by what they do — *Works without VPN*,
  *Through VPN*, *Blocked* — each with its icon and caption. A locked rule shows a lock and an
  "On" its switch cannot change; the others are the customer's to turn on or off. Below the cards,
  if you allow it, customers add their own domains with Direct, Through VPN, or Block. A domain
  covers its subdomains; a pasted link keeps only the domain, and a customer's own rule for a
  domain wins over a service rule.

## Service rules

The **Rules** tab lists the cards customers see, in the order they see them. Each rule has a mode:

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Always (locked)** | Applies to everyone. The card shows a lock and the customer cannot turn it off |
| **On by default** | Applies until the customer turns it off |
| **Off by default** | Applies once the customer turns it on |
| **Hidden** | Neither applies nor shows |

Your own rules are lists of domains, each sending them direct, through the VPN, or nowhere — a
"Gosuslugi" card that is always on with the caption "Opens even under whitelists, no VPN needed",
say. Five rules are built in and carry their own matchers:

- **Russian sites** — the geosite Russian category, direct. Whole zones (.ru, .su, .рф) and Russian
  IP ranges stay in the tunnel: foreign services hosted in Russia would otherwise leave directly, which
  a whitelisted mobile network blocks, and IP checkers would show the customer's own address.
- **AI assistants** — ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude go through the customer's servers where
  Remnawave's geocheck found them working, whichever server the customer picked; the card says
  how many servers each one has. With no check on record the rule changes nothing.
- **Torrents** — BitTorrent never goes through the VPN. Sent direct, the rule is a switch:
  on, torrents download directly at full speed; off, they are blocked, for customers who would
  rather not show their own address to a swarm. Hidden, or set to block, torrents are blocked
  for everyone.
- **Ads and trackers** — the geosite advertising category, blocked.
- **No QUIC** — UDP 443 blocked, so browsers fall back to TCP.

A built-in rule left without a name or caption uses the panel's own words in the customer's
language. Rules can be reordered with the arrows; a built-in one can be hidden but not deleted.

Each card can have an icon: upload a PNG, JPEG, WebP, or SVG under 128 KB, or, with
`APP_LOGO_DEV_TOKEN` set, type a domain and **Find logo** fetches it from
[logo.dev](https://www.logo.dev). The icon is stored in Omniflow and served from your own domain,
so customers' browsers never call a third party. An SVG that could run a script is refused.

An installation that saved settings before rules existed keeps behaving as it did: its
always-direct and always-blocked domain lists become two locked rules, and its three defaults
become the built-in rules' modes.

## Outage messages

With **Outage messages in the bot** on, the bot tells customers when every server of a country
they use has been down longer than the minutes you set, and again when it is back. Customers who
hid that country are left out, and an outage that ends before the message goes out sends
nothing. Customers can turn these messages off under **Servers** in their notification settings.

## Analytics

The **Analytics** tab sums up what customers chose: how many customized their subscription, use
whitelist mode, turned the bypass off, or pinned a server (and which), which service rules they
changed, and which countries they hid or kept out of Fastest. Domains that at least three
subscriptions added for themselves are listed with **Make a rule**, which turns one into a
service rule draft. Nothing here names a customer. The dashboard shows the module's state and the
headline numbers on a card of its own.

Choices are saved per subscription and can be reset to your defaults. They are part of the
customer's privacy export.

## Limits worth knowing

- The ad-blocking rule needs the app's geosite database. Happ and v2RayTun ship it; an app without
  it may refuse the config, which is why the rule is off by default.
- INCY stalls on a config that names the geosite database, so it gets the same groups with
  geosite rules left out: Russian sites and ad blocking do not apply there, and everything else goes
  through the tunnel.
- Omniflow is in the path of every refresh while the module is on. If it is down, apps cannot
  refresh, but configurations they already have keep working.
- The link answers 600 requests a minute per address; a carrier's NAT can put many customers behind
  one.
