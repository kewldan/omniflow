## Gifts

A gift is bought by one customer and redeemed by another. It may be a
subscription, an add-on, or wallet credit.

### Whose history it appears in

The order belongs to the sender and is never copied into the recipient's
history. `orders.user_id` is the sender; the entitlement or ledger entry the
claim produces carries the recipient. That is what keeps a gift out of the
recipient's payment history while still giving them what was bought.

### Claim codes

A code is sixteen Crockford base-32 characters — no `I`, `L`, `O`, or `U`, so
nothing in it is visually ambiguous and no accidental word appears in it. Eighty
bits, combined with the attempt ceiling and the endpoint's rate limit, makes
guessing one impractical.

Only the SHA-256 of the code is stored. A database read never yields a
redeemable code. The last four characters are kept separately as a hint, which
is enough for a sender to tell two of their own gifts apart and for support to
confirm which gift a conversation is about, and not enough to redeem one.

Input is normalised before lookup: separators are stripped and the three
Crockford substitutions (`I`/`L` to `1`, `O` to `0`) are applied, because those
are the characters a person copying by hand reliably confuses. Anything else is
rejected rather than guessed at.

### Redemption rules

Redemption is exactly once by construction: the claim statement only matches a
gift that is deliverable and unexpired, so a second claim of the same code
updates no row.

The refusal reasons are checked in a deliberate order:

1. **Attempt ceiling.** After ten failed presentations a code stops answering
   anything at all, rather than continuing to leak which failure it is. The
   count is durable state on the row, so it survives a restart and cannot be
   reset by waiting out a rate-limit window.
2. **Status.** A gift whose payment has not settled answers "not yet" rather
   than a refusal. A claimed, revoked, refunded, or expired gift is refused.
3. **Expiry**, even if the sweeper has not run yet.
4. **Self-claim.** A sender cannot redeem their own gift: routing money through
   the gift path would otherwise reach a promotion or reward they are not
   eligible for.
5. **Named recipient.** A gift addressed to a Telegram identifier is claimable
   only by that identifier.

### Revocation and refunds

An operator may revoke a gift that is pending, deliverable, or expired. A
claimed gift is deliberately **not** revocable: the recipient already holds what
it bought, and taking that back is a refund decision made against their
entitlement or ledger, with its own permission and its own record.

Only a gift that was never redeemed is refundable to its sender. Refunding a
claimed gift would mean the sender's money back and the recipient's entitlement
intact, which is exactly the abuse this rule prevents.

Revoking takes a row lock first, so a revoke and a claim arriving at the same
moment serialise: whichever commits first wins and the other sees a state its
guarded update refuses.

## Digital goods

A digital good is not VPN access. Nothing in this area creates, modifies, reads,
or depends on a Remnawave entitlement, and a delivery failure here can never
disturb a subscription.

### Provider contract

The adapter interface is small on purpose — quote, deliver, poll, balance, and a
supported-kinds check. Pricing, retry policy, refunds, and the customer record
all sit outside it, so adding a second provider means implementing five methods
rather than re-deciding how the shop works.

Fragment is the only implementation that ships in v0.7. It is not special-cased
anywhere: a product names its provider by slug, and the adapter is selected from
that.

### Pricing

What the customer pays is derived from what the provider charges: a markup in
basis points, then a rounding rule, both operator-configured per product.

| Rounding | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `none`, `up_minor` | The exact amount |
| `up_unit` | Up to a whole major unit |
| `up_ten_units` | Up to a multiple of ten major units |
| `up_hundred_units` | Up to a multiple of a hundred major units |

Every mode rounds up, and the markup itself rounds up rather than truncating. A
shop that loses a minor unit on every sale is a shop whose configured margin is
not the margin it earns.

`fixed_amount_minor` opts out of derivation entirely for an operator who would
rather publish one price and absorb the variance.

Because a provider's rate can move, a quote carries an expiry. `quote_ttl_seconds`
sets it when the adapter does not publish one of its own, and a quote that has
gone stale produces a new quote rather than a charge at the old number.

Both sides of the quote are stored on the order: what the provider asked and
what the customer was shown. Their difference is the realised margin, which is
why the finance view can report it without recomputing a markup that may since
have changed.

### Recipients

The recipient is a Telegram username and nothing else. No display name, no
numeric identifier, no profile data: delivery needs the username and support
needs to be able to answer "where did it go", and neither needs more.

Input is normalised from the forms people actually paste — a bare handle, an
`@` prefix, a `t.me/` link, a link with a query string — and validated against
Telegram's own rule of five to thirty-two characters beginning with a letter.

### Delivery

One delivery row per order, enforced by the primary key. That single constraint
is what makes double delivery impossible: a replayed job, a duplicated webhook,
and a retried worker all find the row that already exists and act on its recorded
state instead of submitting a second time. The idempotency key travels to the
provider as well, which is the provider-side half of the same guard.

A failure is classified, and the class decides what happens next:

| Class | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `retryable` | Retried on the backoff schedule |
| `provider_balance` | Retried, and raises an alert — the operator can top up |
| `provider_unavailable` | Retried; an outage rather than a rejection |
| `recipient_invalid` | Refunded — nothing the worker does will make the username resolve |
| `permanent` | Refunded |

Retry and refund partition the classes exactly: every terminal failure refunds,
and no failure is both retried and refunded. An unrecognised provider error code
is treated as retryable, because retrying an order that was permanently rejected
wastes a few attempts whereas refunding one that would have succeeded loses the
delivery.

Backoff doubles from a minute to a ceiling of an hour, and six attempts — a
little over two hours — is where a retryable failure becomes a refund. The
refund is an ordinary wallet credit, so it appears in the customer's wallet
history exactly like any other.

### Spend controls

A provider carries a balance, a low-balance threshold, and a rolling spend
ceiling measured in provider cost rather than customer price — the ceiling
protects the operator's funding source, and that is what the provider actually
draws down. A zero ceiling means no ceiling.

Credentials are sealed with `APP_DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, are never returned by the
API after they are written, and are excluded from diagnostics.
