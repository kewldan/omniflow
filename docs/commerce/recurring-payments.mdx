---
title: Recurring payments
description: Saved payment methods, auto-renew consent, and the bounded dunning schedule.
---

Automatic charging is the one place in the product where money moves with nobody
watching. Three rules govern it, and all three are enforced rather than
documented.

## Auto-renew is opt-in

There is no default, no inherited setting, and no "enabled because the plan
supports it". A setting carries the instant the customer agreed, and a setting
with no consent record is not chargeable however it came to be enabled — the
money-moving decision depends on the evidence of agreement, not on a boolean
that happens to be true.

Existing rows from v0.5 were backfilled with the moment the setting was last
written, because those rows were created by a customer explicitly enabling
auto-renew in the bot.

## A provider must be able, and permitted

Two gates, both required:

- The **adapter** declares whether it can bind a payment method at all. This is
  compiled in, through `payments.Capabilities.Recurring`.
- The **operator's own merchant account** must have passed a capability test,
  recorded in `payment_provider_settings`. Several acquirers grant card binding
  per merchant rather than per integration, so the adapter's word alone is not
  enough.

The operator switch can only narrow what the adapter declares, never widen it.
The database refuses `recurring_enabled = true` alongside anything but a passing
test, and the API refuses to enable it for an adapter that does not declare the
capability — so a row cannot claim a capability the integration does not have.

## Saved methods hold no instrument

A saved method is a provider-issued token and a masked label the provider
returned. Omniflow neither parses nor derives that label, and no column can hold
a card number, an expiry, or a verification value.

Re-presenting the same provider token refreshes the existing row rather than
creating a second one: the provider still holds one binding. Removing a method
marks it revoked and keeps the row, so a historical charge still resolves to the
method that made it.

Revoking a method does not cancel auto-renew. The settings row keeps its consent
record and simply stops being chargeable, which the renewal path reports as a
failure the customer is told about — rather than silently switching to a funding
source they did not choose.

## Lead time

A renewal is attempted ahead of expiry, three days by default and at most
fourteen. The default is not arbitrary: the whole retry schedule below spans
roughly two and a half days, so it finishes before access actually lapses. A
customer whose card was briefly declined is not cut off while a retry is still
pending.

## Dunning

A failed charge retries a bounded number of times and then stops.

| Attempt | Delay |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | immediate |
| 2 | 4 hours |
| 3 | 12 hours |
| 4 | 24 hours |

Hours rather than minutes, because the reasons an automatic charge fails — an
expired card, a daily limit, a bank hold — are resolved by a person in hours.

Not every failure notifies the customer. The first is usually resolved by the
retry, and a message per attempt trains a customer to ignore the one that
matters; the second failure and the final abandonment are the two that change
what the customer has to do.

After the fourth failure the cycle is abandoned, the settings move to
`suspended`, and the customer is handed back to manual renewal. Consent is not
discarded — the worker simply stops picking the row up.

### Cycle keys

Attempts are unique per `(cycle_key, attempt)`, where the cycle key identifies
one subscription's one renewal period: the entitlement identifier and its end
instant to the second, in UTC. The same expression is computed in SQL by the
query that finds due renewals, so the worker and the uniqueness constraint agree
on what "this renewal" is. A replayed job inserts nothing and reads back the
attempt that already exists.

## What the panel shows

The failed-charge queue under **Finance → Failed charges** is a review surface,
not a control surface. Nothing there re-triggers a charge: the customer has
already been told to renew, and charging them again from a panel click would be
an attempt they did not ask for.

A customer's saved methods and charge history appear on their profile, gated on
`finance.read`. An operator with `finance.write` may revoke a method on the
customer's behalf.

## What the worker actually does

`internal/renewal` is the only place in Omniflow where money moves with nobody
watching, and its design follows from that. The rules — whether a charge is
allowed, when the next attempt falls, when to give up, when to tell the customer
— live in `internal/recurring`, which has no database in it and is unit-tested
on its own. The worker owns the transaction boundaries and the provider calls.

One cycle takes the customer's money at most once, and that rests on three
mechanisms rather than on care.

**The schedule is a table.** `dunning_attempts` carries the attempt number, its
due time, and its outcome, with a unique key on `(cycle_key, attempt)`. A
restarted worker resumes from it rather than starting over, and a duplicated
pass inserts nothing.

**Every attempt on a cycle shares one order.** The order's idempotency key is
derived from the cycle key, so a retry finds the order the previous attempt
opened instead of billing for a second period.

**An outstanding payment blocks a new charge.** If the provider has been asked
and has not answered, the attempt is deferred rather than failed: the honest
reading of silence is "unknown", and charging again on unknown is how a customer
gets billed twice.

Consent is re-checked at the moment of charging rather than at the moment of
scheduling, because the second is the one that moves money. A customer who
turned auto-renew off in between is not charged.

## What the customer controls

The bot states the three things somebody needs to know before agreeing to a
charge they will not be present for: what plan, from where, and how long before
expiry. Enabling auto-renew is what records consent — the worker checks that
timestamp rather than the flag, so a row written by an import or a migration can
never produce a charge.

Saved methods have their own screen showing the provider's own masked label,
which is all Omniflow has. Removing the last method falls auto-renew back to the
wallet and suspends it rather than leaving a customer consented to a charge that
can no longer be made; the consent record stays, so re-adding a card does not
mean agreeing again.

A wallet-funded renewal spends the balance and a card-funded one leaves it
alone. Silently draining a customer's wallet because their card is on file would
be a different agreement from the one they made.

## Which failures the customer hears about

Not every one. The first decline is usually resolved by the retry, and a
notification per attempt teaches a customer to ignore them. The second failure
and the final abandonment are the two that change what the customer has to do,
and those are the two that are sent.

The decision is stored on the attempt rather than recomputed in the notifier's
query, so the rule lives in one place. The message says whether access
continues and what to do; the failure code stays on the attempt row where
support can read it, because "insufficient_funds" is operator vocabulary.
