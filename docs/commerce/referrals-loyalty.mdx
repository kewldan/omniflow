## Referral review

v0.4 built the referral programme: attribution, qualification, and a wallet
reward through the ledger. What it had no answer for is the case an operator
actually has to handle — somebody gaming it.

### Signals ask for a person; they never punish

An abuse signal holds a referral pair for review and marks it `held`. It cannot
reject anything on its own.

That is the whole policy. An automatic system that punishes customers silently
is one nobody can explain to the customer it punished, so a heuristic's only
power here is to ask for a human. Signals record what they saw in terms an
operator can check — counts and identifiers, never a raw device fingerprint or
an IP address, because a review surface is not a reason to widen what is
retained.

### Rejecting reverses through the ledger

Rejecting a pair reverses every live reward with a compensating ledger
transaction. It does not delete the original or edit it to zero.

The ledger is append-only, and "this was granted and then taken back, by this
operator, for this reason" is a different fact from "this was never granted" —
the customer saw the balance either way, and the record has to say so.

A rejection requires a reason, because it takes money back from a customer and
the reason is what an operator quotes to them.

<Warning>
  **A reversal can take a balance negative.** That is deliberate. Refusing to
  reverse because the money has already been spent would make an abusive
  referral profitable as long as the reward is spent quickly. A negative balance
  is visible, explicable from the ledger, and recoverable.
</Warning>

Referral review sits behind `risk.write` rather than a marketing permission.
Deciding that somebody gamed a programme and taking their reward back is an
adverse decision about a customer, which is what that permission governs
everywhere else in the product.

## Loyalty

### Definitions are versioned and immutable

A published loyalty definition is never edited. Editing publishes the next
version, and the old one keeps explaining the assignments made under it.

A customer who reached gold under one set of thresholds should not silently fall
out of it because somebody changed the numbers. The standing stores the metric
the decision was made on and the version in force, so "why am I silver?" has an
answer that does not depend on what the thresholds say today.

### Demotion is slower than promotion

A customer who qualifies is promoted immediately. One who falls below keeps
their tier for the configured grace period first, and recovering inside that
window clears the clock rather than leaving them permanently one quiet month
from demotion.

A loyalty programme that demotes somebody for one slow month punishes exactly
the customers it was built to keep.

A zero grace period demotes at once. That is a legitimate configuration rather
than a bug.

### What a tier is measured on

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `spend` | Settled spend in the configured currency over the window |
| `orders` | Paid orders over the window |
| `tenure` | Days since the customer's first entitlement started |

All three are facts Omniflow already records. That is deliberate: a loyalty
model that needs new tracking is a loyalty model that needs new consent.

### Publication rules

Two things a `CHECK` constraint cannot express are refused by the domain:

- The lowest tier must start at zero, so every customer reaches one. A
  programme with no floor would leave a new customer in no tier at all.
- A higher tier must not be worth less than a lower one. That is representable
  and is certainly a mistake.

### Evaluation is reproducible

`Evaluate` takes the clock as an argument and has no database in it, so the same
inputs always produce the same tier. A loyalty decision nobody can reproduce is
one nobody can defend when a customer disputes it.

It is also idempotent: history is written only when the standing actually
changed, so a sweep running hourly does not produce a history entry every hour.

Every change of standing is kept — a customer asking why they were demoted
deserves an answer, and an operator correcting a mistake needs to see what the
previous state was.

### The discount rounds down

Rounding should never favour the operator at the customer's expense, and here
the operator is the one giving the discount. This is the opposite of the
markup rule in the digital-goods shop, and for the same reason.
