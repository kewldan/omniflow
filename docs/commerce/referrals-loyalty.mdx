title: Referrals and loyalty
description: Configure what an invite pays, understand exactly when a referral qualifies, and publish loyalty tiers a customer cannot silently fall out of
---

Two schemes sit side by side here and are deliberately unrelated. A **referral**
pays a customer wallet credit for bringing somebody who then buys. A **loyalty
tier** places a customer on a ladder derived from what they have already spent,
ordered, or how long they have been around. One moves money, the other does not.

Both are configured under `/admin/marketing`, though only the referral programme
has a form there; loyalty definitions are published through `/v1/panel`.

## Configuring the referral programme

`referral_programs` is a singleton row — the primary key is a boolean that must
be true. A second programme would mean two answers to "what does an invite
earn?", and the customer would already have been told one of them.

Open `/admin/marketing` and scroll to the bottom card. Editing needs
**`settings.write`**, not a marketing permission: changing what an invite pays is
a commercial decision with a bill attached, so it sits beside the other things
that cost the installation money.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `enabled` | Master switch for reward granting. Attribution still happens when it is off |
| `currency` | Currency both rewards are denominated in; exactly three upper-case letters |
| `inviterRewardMinor` | What the inviter earns, in integer minor units; `0` disables that side |
| `inviteeRewardMinor` | What the invited customer earns; `0` disables that side |
| `qualification` | `first_paid_order`, the only rule implemented |
| `inviterRewardCap` | Maximum rewarded invitations per inviter; blank means no cap |
| `attributionValidityDays` | How long an attribution can still qualify |
| `rewardExpiryDays` | Optional expiry on the wallet credit granted |
| `termsUrl` | Programme terms shown to customers |

An installation that never configured a programme reads back the disabled
defaults — `RUB`, `first_paid_order`, 90 days validity, no cap — rather than a
missing-row error.

Beneath the form sits the programme's record to date: attributed, qualified,
rejected, and the money rewarded. It is placed there so a change is made against
evidence rather than against a guess.

### What the API refuses, all with 422

- A `qualification` outside `first_paid_order`. It is a closed set because a
  free-text field would be a promise nothing enforces.
- A currency that is not exactly three upper-case letters.
- A negative reward on either side.
- **Enabled with both rewards at zero.** That would be a scheme that promises
  customers something and pays nothing, which is worse than having no scheme:
  the invites still go out. The panel shows a red "pays nothing" alert and
  disables Save, so an operator learns this before pressing the button rather
  than after.
- `attributionValidityDays` at or below zero, and — when present at all — an
  `inviterRewardCap` or `rewardExpiryDays` at or below zero.

Saving writes the audit action `referral.program.saved` under the marketing
category.

## What actually qualifies a referral

### Attribution comes first, and only once

A customer's referral code is ten base-32 characters matching `^[A-Z0-9]{10}$`,
minted lazily the first time they open their referral screen. The same generator runs in
the bot and in the web panel, so a code minted on one surface is attributable on
the other.

`referral_attributions` is keyed on the **invited** customer, inserted with
`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. Attribution is therefore immutable and first-write-wins:
whoever invited a customer is settled the moment they arrive, and no later
`/start` link rewrites it. A self-referral is impossible by construction — the
table carries a `CHECK` that the two customers differ, and the insert also refuses
a code owned by the customer using it.

<Warning>
  **Only the Telegram path attributes today.** Attribution is recorded from a
  `/start` payload in the bot. The referral link the customer is given points at
  `<APP_PUBLIC_URL>/account/sign-in?ref=<CODE>`, but the web sign-in page reads
  only its `error` parameter and no `/v1/account` route accepts a referral code,
  so a web-originated invite is never attributed. Share the bot link if the
  programme is meant to pay.
</Warning>

### Qualification runs inside the settling transaction

Qualification and granting happen in the same database transaction that settles
the qualifying order. That is what makes them exactly-once: a retried webhook, a
replayed Stars update, or a manual re-approval cannot credit a wallet twice.

All of the following must hold:

1. The programme is enabled and its `qualification` is `first_paid_order`.
2. The order buys subscription time: `purchase`, `extension`, `renewal`,
   `upgrade`, or `downgrade`. A wallet top-up, a shop purchase, a gift bought for
   somebody else, and an access code a distributor paid for are not looked at —
   they neither qualify the referral nor, by settling first, stop a later
   subscription order from qualifying it.
3. The order reached `paid` or `fulfilled`.
4. A payment provider actually settled money — `paid_minor` net of
   `wallet_minor` is positive — so a fully wallet-funded order does not mint new
   value out of a referral. The wallet may hold this very referral's invitee
   credit; only money from outside qualifies.
5. It is the customer's **first** settled subscription order. Nothing earlier of
   theirs with one of those operations sits in `paid`, `fulfilled`,
   `partially_refunded`, or `refunded`.
6. The attribution is still inside `attributionValidityDays`.
7. The inviter is under `inviterRewardCap`, counted over the rewards they already
   hold as an inviter.
8. That role has not already been granted for this pair.

A disabled programme still records attributions; it simply grants nothing. Turning
it on later does not retroactively pay for orders that already settled.

### Reward-once, and what a grant writes

`referral_rewards` is unique on `(referred_user_id, role)`, where role is
`inviter` or `invitee`. Each grant writes, in the same transaction:

- a balanced double-entry ledger transaction — a `customer_wallet` credit against
  a `platform_clearing` debit — with the idempotency key
  `referral:<invited customer>:<role>`;
- the `referral_rewards` row, inserted with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`;
- an optional `expires_at` derived from `rewardExpiryDays`;
- an audit event, `referral.reward_granted`;
- `qualified_at` and `qualifying_order_id` stamped once on the attribution.

Qualification returns an empty result rather than an error when a reward has
already been granted, so a retried job is a no-op instead of a duplicate credit.
See [The wallet and the ledger](/commerce/wallet-ledger) for how the credit
behaves once it exists.

The invitee reward goes to the customer who placed the order; the inviter reward
goes to whoever invited them.

## Referral review and reversal

Deciding that somebody gamed the programme and taking their reward back is an
adverse decision about a customer, so the review routes sit behind the **risk**
permissions rather than marketing ones — the same permissions that govern the
blocklist.

| Route | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/panel/referrals/review?state=&signalled=true&pageSize=` | `risk.read` |
| `POST /v1/panel/referrals/review/{customerID}` | `risk.write` |

`customerID` is the **invited** customer, because that is the attribution's key.
The body is `{state, note}` where state is `clear`, `held`, or `rejected`.

<Note>
  There is no panel screen for either route. Review is done through `/v1/panel`
  requests, and there is currently nothing that raises a signal automatically —
  the `held` state and the `signalled=true` filter are reachable only by an
  operator setting them.
</Note>

### Signals ask for a person; they never punish

A signal holds a referral pair for review and moves it from `clear` to `held`. It
can never move a pair to `rejected` on its own.

That is the whole policy. An automatic system that punishes customers silently is
one nobody can explain to the customer it punished, so the heuristic's only power
is to ask for a human. A signal records what it saw in terms an operator can
check — counts and identifiers, never a device fingerprint or an IP address,
because a review surface is not a reason to widen what is retained.

### Rejecting reverses through the ledger

Rejecting a pair walks every reward on it that has not already been reversed and
writes a compensating `correction` ledger transaction — a wallet debit against
platform clearing — keyed `referral-reversal:<rewardID>`. It then stamps
`reversed_at`, `reversed_by`, `reversal_reason`, and the reversal's ledger
transaction id on the reward row. A `CHECK` constraint guarantees a reward cannot
read as reversed without the money having moved back.

It does not delete the original or edit it to zero. The ledger is append-only, and
"this was granted and then taken back, by this operator, for this reason" is a
different fact from "this was never granted" — the customer saw the balance either
way, and the record has to say so.

A rejection **requires a non-empty note**, because it takes money back from a
customer and the reason is what an operator quotes to them.

<Warning>
  **A reversal can take a wallet balance negative.** That is deliberate. Refusing
  to reverse because the money has already been spent would make an abusive
  referral profitable as long as the reward is spent quickly. A negative balance
  is visible, explicable from the ledger, and recoverable.
</Warning>

A reversal also returns the inviter's cap slot, because the count that drives the
cap only sees live rewards. The decision is audited as `panel.referral.reviewed`
under the risk category, with the state and the note.

## Loyalty

### Definitions are versioned and immutable

`loyalty_programs` rows are versioned, and there is no update route at all.
Publishing a change means publishing the next version; the old one keeps
explaining the assignments made under it. At most one programme may be enabled,
enforced by a partial unique index, and publishing with `enable: true` disables
every other programme in the same statement.

A customer who reached gold under one set of thresholds should not silently fall
out of it because somebody changed the numbers. A standing stores the metric the
decision was made on and the version in force, so "why am I silver?" has an answer
that does not depend on what the thresholds say today.

Publishing is API-only:

```json
POST /v1/panel/loyalty/programs
{
  "metric": "spend",
  "currency": "RUB",
  "windowDays": 365,
  "graceDays": 30,
  "enable": true,
  "tiers": [
    {"code": "bronze", "nameEn": "Bronze", "nameRu": "Бронза", "threshold": 0, "discountBps": 0},
    {"code": "silver", "nameEn": "Silver", "nameRu": "Серебро", "threshold": 500000, "discountBps": 500},
    {"code": "gold",   "nameEn": "Gold",   "nameRu": "Золото", "threshold": 2000000, "discountBps": 1000}
  ]
}
```

The new version number is one above the highest already published. `POST` needs
`marketing.write`; `GET /v1/panel/loyalty/programs` needs `marketing.read`.

### What a tier is measured on

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `spend` | Sum of `paid_minor` over `paid` and `fulfilled` orders in the programme currency, inside the window |
| `orders` | Count of those same orders |
| `tenure` | Days since the customer's earliest entitlement started; the window is irrelevant |

All three are facts Omniflow already records. That is deliberate: a loyalty model
that needs new tracking is a loyalty model that needs new consent.

### Publication rules the domain refuses

Two of these a `CHECK` constraint cannot express, and all of them are 422:

- No tiers at all.
- The lowest tier's threshold is not zero, so some customers would reach no tier
  at all. Every customer must have one.
- A higher tier worth less than a lower one — representable, and certainly a
  mistake.
- An unsupported metric, or a non-positive window for a windowed metric.

The schema adds its own bounds: `discountBps` between 0 and 10000, tier codes and
thresholds unique within a programme, `graceDays` at or above zero, `windowDays`
above zero, and a three-letter upper-case currency.

### Evaluation is reproducible, and demotion is slower than promotion

`Evaluate` takes the clock as an argument and touches no database, so the same
inputs always produce the same tier. A loyalty decision nobody can reproduce is
one nobody can defend when a customer disputes it.

The rules, in order:

1. No current tier — take the earned tier.
2. The held tier belongs to a superseded version — re-place the customer under
   the definition in force.
3. Earned threshold at or above the held one — take the earned tier and clear the
   grace clock; the customer is not being carried.
4. Below the held tier with a zero grace period — demote at once. That is a
   legitimate configuration rather than a bug.
5. Below, with no clock running — keep the tier and start the clock at
   `now + graceDays`. Nothing is written to history, because nothing changed.
6. Below, clock running and not elapsed — keep the tier.
7. Below, clock elapsed — demote.

A loyalty programme that demotes somebody for one quiet month punishes exactly the
customers it was built to keep, which is why the clock exists and why recovering
inside the window clears it.

Evaluation is idempotent: `loyalty_standing_history` is appended only when the
standing actually changed, so an operator pressing the button twice produces one
entry rather than two. History entries are written with the reason `evaluation`.

<Warning>
  **Nothing evaluates automatically.** There is no sweep, no scheduled job, and no
  hook on order settlement. The only caller is
  `POST /v1/panel/customers/{customerID}/loyalty/evaluate` (`marketing.write`),
  one customer at a time, and it has no panel screen. A customer is placed on the
  ladder only when somebody calls that route for them, and their standing is a
  snapshot of the moment it was called.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **A tier's `discountBps` is displayed and never charged.** No pricing, checkout,
  quote, or promotion path reads a loyalty tier. The discount figure is shown to
  the customer on `/account/referrals` and to operators on the customer record,
  and that is all it does today. Use a [promotion](/commerce/catalog-promotions)
  if a discount needs to reach a price.
</Warning>

### What the customer sees

`/account/referrals` renders the ladder, the customer's rung, the metric the
decision was made on, the distance to the next rung, and a percentage through the
current band. It reads the tier the standing **names** rather than recomputing
one, because a customer inside their grace period holds a tier their current
metric no longer earns, and recomputing on page load would demote them the moment
they opened the page.

The payload answers `enabled: false` when no programme is published or the
programme has no tiers, and `enabled: true, evaluated: false` when the customer's
standing belongs to a superseded version — the honest answer being that the
placement is pending re-evaluation, not that they have no tier.

The referral summary on the same screen reports invited, qualified, pending, and
rejected counts taken from the rows rather than derived by subtraction, because
an attribution can be qualified and later rejected on review and a subtraction
would report that pair twice. Money earned and money reversed are reported
separately, so a customer whose total dropped can see why rather than guess.

Nothing in that projection carries the invited customer's identifier. An inviter
is entitled to know that an invite paid out, not to a list of who their friends
are.
