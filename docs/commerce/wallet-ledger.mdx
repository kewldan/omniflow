---
title: Wallet and ledger
description: The double-entry model behind every balance, why entries are append-only, how credit expires, and what an operator must supply to move a customer's money
---

Omniflow does not store a wallet balance. It stores movements, and a balance is what you get when
you add them up. Every movement is a `ledger_transactions` row with at least two `ledger_entries`
that sum to zero per currency, and no code path anywhere updates or deletes either table.

That shape is chosen for one reason: a balance has to be explainable. A stored number that somebody
adjusted is a number with no history; a sum of movements can always answer "why is it this, and
what changed it". Corrections are new compensating pairs, never edits, so the answer survives the
correction.

## The three accounts

| Account type | What it holds | Names a customer? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `customer_wallet` | one customer's credit, per currency | always |
| `platform_clearing` | the platform's own account — revenue lands here, and credit the platform already holds moves out of it | never |
| `provider_clearing` | money sitting at a payment provider | never |

The table CHECK makes those rules structural: a `customer_wallet` entry without a customer, or a
clearing entry with one, cannot be written. Before any transaction is inserted, the domain also
refuses fewer than two entries, a zero amount, a malformed currency, and any currency whose entries
do not sum to zero.

Money is an `int64` count of minor units plus an uppercase three-letter ISO code. Floating point
cannot represent 0.10 exactly, and a drift of one minor unit in a ledger that must sum to zero is
an unbalanced ledger, so nothing in the schema, the domain or the API ever carries a decimal
amount. Requests and responses use `amountMinor` with `currency`; the currency exponent comes from
a fixed table rather than a hard-coded 100, so a zero-decimal currency such as `XTR`, `JPY` or `KRW`
is never scaled by the wrong factor.

## Transaction types

`ledger_transactions.type` is a closed set: `credit`, `debit`, `payment`, `refund`,
`referral_reward`, `correction`, `expiration`. Each row also carries a `reference_type` and
`reference_id` naming what caused it, an `idempotency_key`, and — for anything a person decided — a
`reason` and an `actor_id`.

Two uniqueness guards make a transaction exactly-once from two directions:

- `idempotency_key` is unique across the whole table;
- `(reference_type, reference_id, type)` is unique, so the same cause cannot produce a second
  transaction of the same kind even under a different key.

## Every movement Omniflow writes

| Cause | Type | Reference | Idempotency key | Entries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An order settled | `payment` | `order` / order ID | `payment:{providerEventID}`, or `wallet:{orderKey}` when the wallet covered it | `customer_wallet −wallet`, `provider_clearing −external`, `platform_clearing +(wallet + external)` |
| A top-up credited | `credit` | `wallet_topup` / order ID | `topup:{orderID}` | `customer_wallet +received`, `provider_clearing −received` |
| A refund succeeded | `refund` | `refund` / refund ID | the caller's refund key | `platform_clearing −amount`, `provider_clearing +amount` |
| An operator adjustment | the operator's chosen type | `operator_adjustment` / the caller's reference | the caller's `Idempotency-Key` | `customer_wallet ±amount`, `platform_clearing ∓amount` |
| A referral reward | `referral_reward` | `referral` / referred customer and role | `referral:{...}` | `customer_wallet +reward` (may carry an expiry), `platform_clearing −reward` |
| A gift claimed as wallet credit | `credit` | `gift` / gift ID | `gift:{giftID}` | `customer_wallet +credit`, `platform_clearing −credit` |
| Wallet credit expired | `expiration` | `ledger_entry` / entry ID | `expiration:{entryID}` | `customer_wallet −amount`, `platform_clearing +amount` |
| A digital-goods delivery failed permanently | `refund` | `goods_order` / order ID | `goods-refund:{orderID}` | `customer_wallet +price`, `platform_clearing −price` |

Two of these are worth reading closely.

A **gift claim** pairs the recipient's credit against `platform_clearing`, not against revenue. The
sender's money was recognised when their own order settled, and recognising it a second time when
the recipient claims would double-count it.

A **top-up** credits `customer_wallet` against `provider_clearing`, because the money genuinely came
from a provider and has not been earned yet. An order settling then moves it from the customer to
`platform_clearing` as revenue. That is why a customer paying entirely from their wallet still
produces a balanced movement with no provider leg.

## Balances

A balance is the plain sum of a customer's `customer_wallet` entries in one currency. Nothing is
filtered out of that sum — not expired credit, not old entries — which is exactly what makes it
deterministic and what makes the customer's history reconcile against it line by line.

<Warning>
  Earlier documentation described balances as the sum of *non-expired* entries. That is not how it
  works, and the difference matters when you are auditing: an expired credit is removed by a
  compensating negative entry written by a background loop, so a balance converges on the expected
  figure once that loop has run rather than the instant the expiry timestamp passes.
</Warning>

The customer-facing screens show three figures per currency, because one number would answer none
of the questions honestly:

| Figure | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Total | what the ledger holds |
| Reserved | what the customer's own `draft` and `pending` orders have already claimed |
| Available | what a new order may actually spend |

Reserved is subtracted with the same arithmetic the order path uses. Without it, two checkouts
opened side by side would each believe the same money was theirs. A customer shown only the total
and then given a smaller amount at checkout has been told the wrong number twice.

## Expiring credit

A ledger entry may carry `expires_at`. Only referral rewards set it, and only when the referral
program configures `reward_expiry_days` — see [Referrals and
loyalty](/commerce/referrals-loyalty).

A loop inside the API process's maintenance ticker runs once a minute, takes up to 500 positive
customer entries whose expiry has passed and which have no `expiration` transaction yet, and writes
one compensating pair for each.

Two decisions in that loop are deliberate:

- Expiry is a **compensating entry**, not a filter on the balance query. The balance stays a plain
  sum, and the expiry itself appears in the customer's history where they can see what happened to
  their credit.
- The amount is **clamped** to the entry amount, the customer's current balance, and what they
  received after that entry. A customer who already spent the credit is not driven negative by its
  expiry.

An entry whose clamped amount comes out at zero still gets its `expiration` transaction, with no
entries under it, which is what stops the loop from re-examining it forever.

<Note>
  The maintenance loop is started only by the API process. An installation running just the bot and
  the worker never expires wallet credit, and expired referral rewards stay spendable.
</Note>

## What a customer sees

- **Web.** `/account/wallet` shows the three figures per currency, the top-up controls, and a
  cursor-paginated history of the customer's own entries with the transaction type and, where a
  correction carried one, the operator's reason. The API is `GET /v1/account/wallet`.
- **Telegram.** **Wallet** shows the balance and the last ten movements with signed amounts, and
  **Top up** starts a funding order.

Operator reasons are shown to customers verbatim. A balance that changed without explanation is
worse than one whose explanation was written for staff, so write adjustment reasons expecting a
customer to read them.

Funding the wallet is [Wallet top-up and saved carts](/commerce/wallet-topup-cart); spending it is
described under wallet-first payment in [Orders, payments and
refunds](/commerce/orders-payments#wallet-credit-is-applied-before-external-payment).

## What an operator sees

`/admin/customers/{customerId}` → **Wallet** reads
`GET /v1/panel/customers/{customerID}/wallet`, and the permission is **`finance.read`**, not
`customers.read`. The split is intentional: a support operator can see that a customer has a
balance without being able to read every movement that produced it.

The panel's wallet tab is read-only. There is no update or delete path for a ledger row anywhere in
the panel, and no panel route that adjusts a balance.

## Operator adjustments

Goodwill credit after a support case, a correction, a manual referral payout — all of these are one
API call, and only that call. There is no `/v1/panel` route and no screen in `/admin` for adjusting
a wallet; the bearer-token API is the whole surface.

```bash
curl -X POST https://example.com/v1/admin/customers/{customerID}/wallet-adjustments \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $APP_OPERATOR_TOKEN" \
  -H "Idempotency-Key: goodwill-2026-08-13-0007" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"amountMinor": 50000, "currency": "RUB", "type": "credit",
       "reference": "ticket-4821", "reason": "Outage compensation",
       "operatorID": "ops@example.com"}'
```

What is required, and why:

<Steps>
  <Step title="A reason, an actor, and a non-zero amount">
    All three are refused if missing. Requiring them is what makes a balance that changed without
    an explanation impossible, and the reason is shown to the customer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A type, with the right sign">
    `credit`, `debit`, `referral_reward`, `correction` or `expiration`. `credit` and
    `referral_reward` must be positive; `debit` and `expiration` must be negative; `correction` may
    be either. A credit written with a negative amount is a typo, not an intention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="An idempotency key">
    Reusing a key with a different type, reference, actor, customer, currency or amount is refused
    with "idempotency key was already used with different wallet parameters". Reusing it with the
    same parameters returns without writing a second transaction, so a retried request is safe.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A balance that stays non-negative">
    A negative adjustment re-reads the balance inside the serializable transaction and refuses with
    "wallet adjustment would create a negative balance" rather than writing a debit the customer
    cannot cover.
  </Step>
</Steps>

On success the transaction and both entries are written, and an audit event `wallet.adjusted` is
appended with category `financial`, outcome `success`, the customer as its target, and the amount
and currency in its metadata. The adjustment then appears in the customer's own wallet history with
its type and reason.

## Retention and privacy

Financial history is never removed by a retention sweep, and anonymising a customer keeps it: an
order that funded a ledger entry cannot be deleted without unbalancing the ledger. What
anonymisation removes is contact and identity data, not the movements that explain a balance. See
[Customer privacy](/customer/privacy) and [Financial
invariants](/commerce/financial-invariants).
