Two things let a customer shape a subscription beyond picking a plan: choosing which squads it uses, and buying extra capacity part-way through a period.

## Plan-scoped squad sets

A plan version carries two squad collections:

- `remnawave_squad_ids` — always assigned to every subscription on that plan;
- `plan_version_squads` — the selectable set, each row carrying a Russian and an English label.

`squad_selection` decides what the customer may do:

| Value | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| `automatic` | Squads are assigned from the plan. A submitted selection is refused. |
| `optional` | The customer may add squads from the offered set, within `max_selectable_squads`. |
| `required` | The customer must pick at least `min_selectable_squads`, and at least one. |

The final squad set for the entitlement is the included squads plus the accepted selection, deduplicated and sorted. Sorting matters: two customers who pick the same squads in a different order must produce byte-identical entitlement state, otherwise drift detection would report a mismatch that is not real.

A selection is validated against the plan on the server. Callback data naming a squad the plan does not offer is refused with `squad_not_offered`, so a crafted callback cannot attach a squad the customer never paid for.

The configurator only appears when a plan actually offers a choice. A plan with `automatic` selection goes straight from the plan screen to payment, exactly as before.

## Add-ons

An add-on adds capacity to a subscription. It can be bought two ways:

- **With the plan**, at checkout or in a saved cart. It is charged on the same order, so one confirmation is one payment, and it covers the whole first period at the catalog price.
- **Mid-period**, against a live entitlement. It is charged on its own order and priced with its version's proration rule against the time the period has left.

Three kinds exist:

| Kind | Adds |
| --- | --- |
| `traffic` | Bytes on top of the entitlement's allowance |
| `devices` | Device slots on top of the entitlement's limit |
| `squads` | Additional Remnawave squads |

An add-on is offered only for plan versions that list it in `plan_version_addons`. Buying one against a plan that does not offer it is refused with `addon_unavailable`.

### Versioned pricing

Add-ons are versioned exactly like plans. `addon_versions` is immutable once an order references it, and `order_addon_lines` stores a snapshot of the version's capacity, price, and proration rule at purchase time. Changing an add-on's price creates a new version; it never rewrites a historical order.

### Proration rules

The proration rule is a property of the add-on version, and it is always explicit. Omniflow never infers a partial price from remaining time unless the add-on says so. It applies only to a mid-period purchase; an add-on bought with the plan covers a full period and is charged at the catalog price whatever its rule says.

| Rule | Charge | Suited to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `full_price` | The catalog price, whatever remains of the period | Capacity that does not decay, such as device slots |
| `remaining_period` | Catalog price × remaining ÷ total, rounded up to the next minor unit | Traffic, where value scales with time |
| `daily_rate` | Catalog price ÷ whole period days, rounded up, × remaining days, rounded up | Add-ons an operator prices per day |

Three properties hold for every rule:

1. A prorated charge never exceeds the catalog price.
2. Rounding is always up, so a partial period never rounds to nothing.
3. A purchase made before the period starts, or against a period with no remaining time, falls back to the full price. Charging zero for capacity that will still be delivered would give it away.

The intermediate multiplication is done in 128 bits. A long period and a large price would overflow a 64-bit product and produce a nonsensical charge.

### Applying an add-on

Settlement folds the purchased capacity into the entitlement itself before the desired provisioning state is computed, so Remnawave receives one combined result rather than a plan followed by a correction:

```text
entitlement.traffic_allowance_bytes += Σ traffic × quantity
entitlement.device_limit            += Σ slots × quantity
entitlement.remnawave_squad_ids      = existing ∪ add-on squads
```

An entitlement with an unlimited allowance stays unlimited: adding capacity to "no limit" is meaningless, so a null limit is left null.

Squads are a set and are never multiplied by quantity. Buying two of the same squad add-on charges twice and grants the same squads once, which is why `max_quantity` on a squad add-on should be 1.

### Idempotent fulfillment

`entitlement_addons` is unique on `(order_id, addon_version_id)`. A replayed settlement finds the row already present, skips it, and leaves the entitlement untouched. Only lines that were actually inserted contribute to the totals, so a partial replay cannot double-count.

A mid-period add-on order enqueues its own fulfillment operation keyed `order:<order id>:addon`, so the operation itself is created once however many times settlement is attempted. An add-on bought with the plan needs no separate operation: it is already part of the entitlement the plan's own `create` operation provisions.

### When there is nothing to attach to

If the target subscription has no live entitlement by the time an add-on settles, the money is not lost and nothing is rolled back. The order stays `paid`, an `addon.no_active_entitlement` audit event is written, and an operator notice is queued so a human can refund or re-apply it. Rolling back a recorded payment would be worse than leaving an operator a clear task.
